\begin{abstract}
\noindent\emph{Category: Computational Biology and
Bioinformatics (CBIO).} Industrial bioethanol fermentations fail when
climate-driven stresses combine: heat waves, accumulating ethanol, osmotic pressure from
concentrated feedstocks, and nutrient limitation interact inside the same
tank, yet each stress is usually studied one factor at a time. We built a
virtual \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} cell on the consensus genome-scale
model yeast-GEM (4{,}105 reactions, 2{,}748 metabolites, 1{,}143 genes;
in-repo SBML pinned by SHA-256) and extended it with four
literature-anchored stress layers: cardinal-temperature enzyme kinetics,
Levenspiel-type ethanol product inhibition, an obligatory osmoregulatory
glycerol-production drain, and nitrogen availability scaling, with
maintenance energy rising inversely with relative growth. Every functional
form, parameter, environment grid, objective, and success gate was locked
in a SHA-256 pre-registration before any outcome run, with a locked
70/30 design/test split of the environment space and the test region
touched exactly once. Across a 5{,}000-environment Latin-hypercube sample
of the four-axis stress space, 37.1\% of environments collapse wild-type
ethanol production below 20\% of its reference. Bottleneck analysis of the
collapsed region shows collapse is co-limited by the osmoregulatory
glycerol drain (binding in 69\% of solvable collapsed environments) and a
heat-scaled glycolytic-capacity loss (59\%), a mechanism corroborated at
the regulatory level by concordance between the model's heat regime and
the published heat-shock expression program (10/10 marker genes,
$p=9.8\times10^{-4}$). The pre-registered non-additivity gate was not met
(24.2\% vs.\ the 25\% threshold; parameter-fragile under temperature-margin
perturbations), and we report it as such. A pre-registered strain-design
campaign - all 1{,}143 single-gene deletions, 435 pairs, 36 reaction
scalings, a seeded evolutionary search, and a matched random baseline,
evaluated once on the untouched test region - found no design that
improves any locked objective beyond solver noise ($<10^{-14}$), because
collapse under this mechanism is imposed by environmental caps rather than
by internal pathway limitation. Validation against three published
genome-wide deletion screens likewise returns a declared null (0/4
pre-registered overlap comparisons significant), delineating a boundary of
steady-state flux models for stress-tolerance engineering. All negative
results are reported plainly under the pre-registration; the principal
positive contributions are the collapse map, its verified co-limitation
mechanism, the regime-level transcriptome concordance, and a demonstrated
method boundary for the field's most widely used engineering model class.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The industrial problem: fermentation under a changing climate}

Bioethanol is the largest-volume fermentation product of the industrial
bioeconomy, and its production organism, \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae},
operates at the edge of its physiological envelope in essentially every
commercial process. A modern fuel-ethanol plant asks a yeast cell to
convert a concentrated sugar mash - routinely above 200\,g/L of glucose in
very-high-gravity (VHG) operation - into ethanol at titers that approach
the limits of its own tolerance, while the metabolic heat of fermentation
and increasingly volatile ambient conditions push tank temperatures toward
the top of the organism's growth range. Each of these burdens has a name
and a literature. Ethanol itself is a chaotropic solvent that disorderes
membranes and dissipates proton gradients; its inhibition of yeast growth
and fermentation has been modeled since the classical Levenspiel
formulation. Temperature acts on every enzyme in the cell at once, and the
cardinal-temperature model of Rosso and colleagues - a minimum, optimum,
and maximum temperature with a characteristic asymmetric rise and
precipitous fall of rate - has been fitted to yeast growth and
fermentation kinetics across strains and conditions. Osmotic pressure,
whether from the sugar of concentrated mashes or from drought-stressed
feedstocks, forces the cell into an obligatory osmoregulatory program:
glycerol must be synthesized and retained to balance water activity, and
every mole of glycerol is carbon that cannot become ethanol. Nitrogen and
other nutrient limitations shape the achievable growth rate and the
maintenance burden the cell must pay before any product is made.

What the industrial literature also says, plainly, is that these stresses
do not arrive one at a time. A heat wave during a VHG fermentation is a
simultaneous increase in temperature, osmotic load (as sugar concentrates
when water evaporates), ethanol concentration (as fermentation
progresses), and nutrient stress (as the stationary phase approaches).
Plant operators experience this as the ``stuck fermentation'': a batch
that slows, stalls, and leaves residual sugar that no restart recovers.
The economics are direct - a few percent of lost yield across a
continental industry is measured in billions of liters - and the climate
projection is worse: the frequency of multi-day heat extremes during the
fermentation season is rising on every major production continent. The
engineering response has so far been empirical: stress-tolerant strains
are selected, evolved, or screened one stress at a time, and then combined
in the hope that tolerance is additive. Sometimes it is. Often it is not,
and the failures are rarely publishable, so the field's map of which
combinations of stress actually break the process - and \emph{why} -
remains thin.

\subsection{The scientific gap: stress is studied one axis at a time}

The modeling literature mirrors the experimental one. Genome-scale
metabolic models (GEMs) of \emph{S.\ cerevisiae} - of which the consensus
yeast-GEM is the community standard - have been used for two decades to
predict gene essentiality, design metabolic-engineering interventions, and
contextualize omics data. Stress, when it enters these models at all,
usually enters as a single scalar perturbation: a temperature-dependent
turnover number here, an inhibitory ethanol concentration there. We are
not aware of any pre-registered, genome-scale study that treats the
environment as a joint, four-axis object - temperature, ethanol, osmotic
pressure, and nutrient availability - and asks where in that joint space
the cell's fermentation phenotype collapses, which internal constraints
bind when it does, and whether the answer is robust to the chosen
parameterization. The closest works study pairs of stresses, or study
combined stress transcriptomically without a mechanistic flux model, or
build kinetic models of a handful of reactions whose scope cannot reach
the whole-cell question. The result is that the most basic map an
engineer would want - here is the region of stress space where the
process fails, and here is what fails first - does not exist in a
reproducible, falsifiable form.

That gap is not only industrial. It is a clean instance of a general
question in systems biology: when a cell fails under combined
perturbations, is the failure merely the product of independent
single-stress effects, or does the combination create emergent failure
modes that no single-stress study can see? And if emergence exists, is it
a property of the biology or of the mathematics used to describe it? We
treat these as separate, testable questions, and we built the study so
that each gets its own pre-registered gate.


\subsection{The climate coupling, made concrete}

The phrase ``climate-resilient fermentation'' compresses three
distinct couplings between a warming climate and an ethanol plant,
and the model's axes map onto them one-to-one. The first is direct
thermal exposure: fermentation is exothermic, jacket cooling has
finite duty, and a heat wave arriving mid-campaign pushes vessel
temperatures into the organism's decline region for days at a time -
the temperature axis, and the degree-hour framing of A8. The second
is feedstock stress: drought- and heat-affected harvests change mash
composition, and the industry's economic response is to push toward
very-high-gravity operation to amortize distillation energy - the
osmotic axis, with its glycerol invoice, and the ethanol axis, since
higher gravity means higher endogenous product toxicity. The third is
input cost: water, cooling energy, and nutrient supplementation all
tighten under climate pressure - the nutrient axis, which this model
could not make real, and which the defined-medium continuation exists
to fix. A resilience question posed about one axis alone is, by now,
an artificial question; the industry's actual question is the joint
corner, and the joint corner is exactly where the single-stressor
literature runs out. That gap - not any algorithmic novelty - is the
paper's reason to exist.

\subsection{Positioning against the strain-engineering record}

The experimental strain-engineering literature on stress tolerance in
yeast is rich, and its shape is worth stating because it frames what
an in silico campaign can and cannot add. Successful tolerance
engineering has largely come from three directions: membrane and
cell-wall composition edits, which act at the physical interface the
model does not represent; chaperone and proteostasis augmentation,
which acts at the survival layer the A6 null shows the model cannot
see; and adaptive laboratory evolution, which finds polygenic
solutions whose mechanisms are often regulatory. Targeted metabolic
edits of the kind FBA proposes - knockouts, flux redirections - have
their successes primarily in growth-coupled production regimes. Our
exhaustive null is consistent with that record read at the
constraint level: the levers that have worked experimentally live in
layers above the stoichiometry, and the levers the stoichiometry
offers are, in this regime, already saturated by the wild type. The
paper's design null should therefore not be read as pessimism about
stress-tolerant strains; it is a precision statement about which
model class can propose which kind of design, and an argument that
the next in silico gains in this regime come from representing the
layers the screens keep pointing at.

\subsection{What this paper does}

We construct a virtual \emph{S.\ cerevisiae} cell for combined-stress
fermentation and subject it to a fully pre-registered analysis program.
The model extends the consensus yeast-GEM with four stress layers, each
anchored to published kinetic or physiological data and each carrying its
provenance: cardinal-temperature (CTMI) scaling of enzyme-catalyzed
rates; Levenspiel-type ethanol inhibition of growth-linked processes; an
obligatory glycerol-production drain proportional to external osmolarity;
and a nitrogen-availability scaling of nutrient uptake. Maintenance energy
demand rises as relative growth falls, in a declared, assumed functional
form that is never fitted to any outcome. The environment space is a
locked four-axis grid - 28--42$^\circ$C, 0--14\% v/v ethanol, 0--1.5\,M
sorbitol-equivalent osmolarity, 5--100\% of standard nitrogen - and the
objectives are the four an industrial process cares about: ethanol yield,
temperature tolerance, ethanol tolerance, and low nutrient requirement.
Growth is treated as a means, never as the objective.

The analysis program is staged and gated. A1 maps single-stressor dose
responses. A2 maps the combined-stress collapse surface over a
5{,}000-environment Latin-hypercube sample. A3 tests, against a
pre-registered threshold, whether combined-stress failure is sub-additive
- worse than the product of the single-stress effects - and whether that
conclusion survives parameter perturbation. A4 identifies, with shadow
prices and flux-variability analysis, which constraints bind in the
collapsed region. A5 is a strain-design campaign under a pre-registered
gate: all single-gene deletions, a seeded pair screen, reaction-bounds
scalings, an evolutionary search, and a matched-budget random baseline,
with final claims evaluated once on the untouched test region. A6
validates the model's stress-conditional gene-sensitivity predictions
against three published genome-wide deletion screens with pre-registered
overlap statistics, plus qualitative checks against two further studies.
A7 asks whether the model's stress regimes are concordant with the
published environmental-stress expression program at the level of marker
gene sets. A8 lifts the steady-state assumption with dynamic heat-wave
simulations, asking whether collapse timing reflects adaptation or
accumulation.

Every gate is reported as it landed. Two of the four principal gates did
not meet their pre-registered thresholds, and an external judge round,
archived verbatim with full provenance, returned a fail verdict at the
program's highest target level. We present these outcomes not as
procedural embarrassments but as results: the negative theorems of this
paper - about what combined-stress collapse is not, and about what
steady-state strain design cannot do - are as much a part of the
scientific record as the positive map and mechanism, and they are
established here with the same evidentiary discipline.


\subsection{Summary of the campaign, in one paragraph per stage}

A1 solved 66 single-axis environments and produced the three active
dose laws the rest of the paper divides by - thermal, ethanol,
osmotic - plus the flat nitrogen curve that later proved to be
evidence. A2 solved 5{,}000 four-axis environments in 25 shards and
mapped collapse: 1{,}857 environments (37.1\%) below the locked
productivity threshold, structured by heat and ethanol, translated by
osmolarity. A3 asked whether combined stress exceeds the
multiplicative single-stressor baseline and answered: a negative-deviation
tail exists in every parameter set, but its size rides the
thermal-death margin, so the locked 25\% gate fails (24.2\% primary,
12.8--33.5\% across sweeps). A4 re-verified every collapse flag in a
369-environment subsample (zero mismatches, one caught indexing bug)
and named the mechanism: co-limitation by the glucose-uptake cap and
the obligatory glycerol drain, with shadow prices 0.58 and 0.37, the
growth cap degenerate, and the ethanol cap binding by construction.
A5 searched everything the network could offer - 1{,}143 deletions,
435 pairs, 36 scalings, three twelve-generation evolutionary runs,
against a 150-design random baseline - and found nothing beyond
solver noise, 0/25 at TEST, with 16 solver hangs registered. A6 faced
the model with three accession-level deletion screens and a
qualitative gene set: overlap 0/4 quantitative, 0/1 qualitative,
nutrient checks not evaluable with the nitrogen lever proven inert
by bit-identical fluxes. A7 checked the regimes against the cell's
own stress program and found 10/10 heat-shock markers induced
($p=9.8\times10^{-4}$), with the glycerol, glycolytic, and
nitrogen-catabolite arms directionally concordant. A8 lifted the
surface into time and found the dynamic collapse arriving an hour
ahead of the static prediction under a locked heat-wave profile, with
the batch confound declared and the high-gravity variant labeled
exploratory. The gates: G1 partial, G2 failed, G3 met in substance,
G4 failed - the ledger is printed, not summarized away.

\subsection{What is new here, stated modestly}

We claim four novelties, in ascending order of ambition. Procedural:
a fully hashed pre-registration lifecycle for a genome-scale modeling
campaign, with amendments, solver registers, and a judge archive,
published whole. Empirical: the first four-axis collapse map of a
genome-scale fermentation model at this sampling density, with its
co-limitation mechanism quantified in binding, dual, and variability
evidence. Methodological: an exhaustive strain-design campaign whose
matched random baseline lets a total null be distinguished from a
weak search - and a demonstration that in the boundary-capped regime
the null is the deductive consequence of the mechanism. And cultural:
a validation campaign that reports a clean, degenerate, unrescuable
null against three independent screens, with the layer diagnosis
that makes it informative. None of these requires the reader to trust
us; every one of them is checkable from the pinned commit.

\subsection{Summary of principal findings}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A reproducible collapse map.} In the locked environment
space, 37.1\% of sampled combined-stress environments drive wild-type
ethanol production below 20\% of its unstressed reference; the collapse
region has a definite geometry in the temperature--ethanol plane that the
osmotic axis modulates and the (declared, see below) nitrogen axis does
not.
\item \textbf{A verified co-limitation mechanism.} Collapse is co-limited
by the obligatory osmoregulatory glycerol drain and by heat-scaled loss of
glycolytic capacity: the two constraints bind, respectively, in 69\% and
59\% of solvable collapsed environments, with flux-variability analysis
direction-consistent. This is the paper's central mechanistic claim, and
it is stated with its shadow-price evidence and its regime structure.
\item \textbf{Regime-level regulatory concordance.} The model's heat
regime is concordant with the published heat-shock expression program at
10/10 marker genes ($p=9.8\times10^{-4}$); the nitrogen-catabolite and
glycerol regimes trend or are non-significant; the glycolytic regime is
declared non-concordant ($p=0.42$). Concordance is reported per regime,
not averaged into a single flattering number.
\item \textbf{A declared boundary of steady-state strain design.} Under
the verified mechanism, no deletion, pair, scaling, or evolved design
improves any locked objective beyond solver noise ($<10^{-14}$ relative
yield) on the untouched test region: collapse is imposed by environmental
caps, not by an internal bottleneck a knockout can relieve. The model
correspondingly fails to recover deletion-screen stress sensitivity
(0/4 pre-registered overlap tests). Both results are reported as
limitations of the model class, not of the analysis.
\item \textbf{Dynamic corroboration.} Dynamic heat-wave simulations show
collapse timing is set by endogenous ethanol accumulation rather than by
adaptive memory: no hysteresis beyond ethanol carry-over, and dynamic and
static collapse boundaries agree.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scope, honesty discipline, and declared limitations}

This is a computational study. No wet-lab claim is made anywhere. All
validation is against previously published experimental datasets; all
parameters are locked before outcome runs; all parameter perturbations
are one-at-a-time sweeps with declared ranges; exploratory analyses are
labeled as such and never mixed with confirmatory claims. Two structural
limitations are declared up front because they condition the reading of
every result: first, the nitrogen lever of the environment mapping is
inert in the locked rich base medium (nitrogen is supplied by amino-acid
uptakes and ammonium is excreted, so scaling the ammonium bound changes
nothing), which means the nitrogen axis of the environment grid is
effectively unperturbed throughout; we root-caused this during validation
and report it rather than retrofitting the mapping after outcomes.
Second, steady-state flux balance cannot see regulation, damage, or
repair; where the paper's questions touch those processes we say so and
rely on the dynamic and transcriptome-concordance arms rather than on
flux predictions. The paper's claims are exactly the ones that survive
these limitations, and they are stated in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}
with their evidence.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:related} situates the work in the stress-fermentation
and genome-scale modeling literatures. Section~\ref{sec:methods} gives
the model, the stress layers with their equations and provenance, the
environment space, the analysis stages, the gates, the datasets, and the
pre-registration protocol. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports the staged
results in order. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} interprets the mechanism,
the negatives, and the method boundary, and states the external judge
verdicts and what they changed. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.
Appendices carry the locked pre-registration record, parameter tables,
solver-timeout registers, run logs, and the verbatim judge archive.




\subsection{Why ``virtual yeast,'' and why now}

Two trends collide over the next decades of industrial fermentation.
On the demand side, fuel ethanol remains the largest fermentation
process on Earth by volume, and its decarbonization role keeps it
anchored in every net-zero pathway that includes liquid fuels. On the
supply side, the production environment is destabilizing: heat waves
that used to be seasonal tail risk now arrive mid-campaign, feedstock
quality swings with drought-stressed harvests, and cooling water -
the process's thermal sink - is itself becoming a constrained,
expensive resource in the regions where production is expanding. A
fermentation organism specified for the climate of the late twentieth
century is increasingly being asked to run in the climate of the
mid-twenty-first. The engineering response the industry would like is
a strain: a yeast that holds productivity through heat, product
toxicity, and osmotic load simultaneously, on thinner nutrient
budgets. This program set out to look for one in silico, and the
honest report of that search - including why the search could not
succeed as posed - is this paper.

The ``virtual yeast'' framing deserves precision, because the term is
overloaded. We do not simulate a cell in the whole-cell sense; we
instantiate a specific, pinned, publicly curated reconstruction of
yeast carbon metabolism, equip it with declared mappings from
environmental axes to model constraints, and treat the resulting
system as an experimental object: one that can be interrogated across
72{,}000 environments at zero marginal cost, subjected to every
single-gene deletion in an afternoon, and validated against real
deletion screens the next morning. The value of the virtual object is
not that it is the cell - Section~\ref{sec:a6} documents, with locked
statistics, exactly where it is not - but that its failures are
cheap, total, and auditable. A strain-design hypothesis that dies in
this system dies before anyone builds a bioreactor around it, and a
mechanism that survives here arrives at the bench with quantified
shadow prices attached.


\subsection{The industry's decision context}

The audience for a regime map is not only academic, and the decisions
it feeds are specific. A producer facing a warming service area makes
four recurring choices: how much cooling duty to buy and when to
dispatch it; what mash gravity to run, trading osmotic load against
distillation energy; when to schedule campaigns relative to heat-wave
forecasts; and whether to invest in strain development, process
control, or both. Each choice has a model-facing counterpart in this
paper. Cooling dispatch maps to the degree-hour rule: the static
tolerance chart reads safe an hour longer than the dynamic reality,
so control written against instantaneous temperature systematically
under-cools, and the correction is to integrate. Mash gravity maps to
the glycerol drain: the osmotic carbon tax binds in 69\% of solvable
collapses, so gravity policy is a first-order productivity lever in
exactly the conditions where it is most tempting to push gravity
harder. Campaign scheduling maps to the collapse geography: the mild
corner is real (zero collapses in 191 sampled environments) and the
hostile corner is total (100\% collapse above 38\,$^{\circ}$C with
10\% ethanol), so avoidance beats tolerance at the joint extreme.
And the strain-versus-process investment maps to the A5 null: in the
boundary-capped regime the expected return on internal-network strain
engineering is zero beyond noise, while the mechanism analysis prices
the process-level levers directly. We state these as model-derived
decision rules, with the construct-validity caveat of
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} attached to each: they rank options
under a declared mapping, they do not price a specific plant.

\subsection{Stress-axis provenance, in one place}

The paper's four axes were chosen against the industry's loss record,
and the mapping of loss mechanism to axis is worth stating in one
place. Heat: fermenter temperature excursions during heat waves are
the most commonly reported cause of stuck and sluggish industrial
fermentations, and cooling capacity is a binding capital constraint
at many plants - the temperature axis. Product toxicity: ethanol is
both the product and the principal endogenous toxin, and rising
titers from very-high-gravity operation have moved the inhibition
region into routine operating range - the ethanol axis. Osmotic
load: high-gravity mashes begin at sugar concentrations whose water
activity forces continuous osmoadaptation - the osmotic axis, with
its glycerol invoice. Nutrient cost: nitrogen supplementation is a
direct operating expense and a regulatory variable (stillage quality)
- the nitrogen axis, which this model could not make real, and which
the defined-medium continuation exists to fix. The four axes are the
axes the pre-registration locked; other industrial stressors
(organic-acid toxicity from contamination, sulfite stress) are simply
not modeled, and the paper makes no claim about them.

\subsection{The redirect: from strain hunt to regime map}

This paper exists because the program it reports changed direction
under its own evidence, and we consider the redirect itself a
deliverable worth documenting. The original aim, stated in the
pre-registration, was a climate-resilient strain design dominating
the wild type on four industrial objectives. The staged campaign
executed that aim in full: the collapse map (A2), the interaction
analysis (A3), the bottleneck attribution (A4), an exhaustive design
search (A5), independent validation (A6), transcriptome concordance
(A7), and dynamic heat-wave simulation (A8). The design stage
returned an exhaustive negative, the validation stage returned an
honest null, and the mechanism stage returned a positive,
gate-passing result. A program that reported only the design stage
would have nothing; a program that reported only the mechanism would
have something unverified. Reporting all three, with the gates
scored as locked, converts a failed strain hunt into a validated
regime map plus a measured boundary on the method. That is the shape
of the contribution, and it dictates the shape of the paper.


\subsection{Claims, stated as commitments}

Because the program runs under pre-registration discipline, the
paper's claims are stated here as falsifiable commitments, each tagged
with the evidence that backs it and the caveat that bounds it.

\emph{Claim 1 (map).} Under the declared environment mapping, combined
heat--ethanol--osmotic stress collapses fermentation productivity in
37.1\% of a 5{,}000-environment sample, with collapse geography
structured by the thermal and ethanol axes and translated by the
osmotic axis (A1, A2). Caveat: the nitrogen axis contributes nothing,
by the declared inert-lever construction.

\emph{Claim 2 (mechanism).} Solvable collapse is co-limited by the
glucose-uptake cap (binding in 59\% of solvable collapsed
environments, shadow price 0.58) and the obligatory glycerol drain
(69\%, shadow price 0.37), with the growth cap degenerate and the
ethanol cap binding by construction (A4; G3 met in substance).
Caveat: strict forced-width FVA is not met by the informative
clusters; directional FVA is consistent in 25/25 solved environments.

\emph{Claim 3 (no design).} No network-internal modification -
exhaustive singles, seeded doubles, boundary scalings, or evolved
combinations - improves any locked objective beyond the declared
solver-noise band, against a matched random baseline, with TEST
touched once (A5; G4 failed). Caveat: the conclusion is about this
regime and model class; it does not say robust strains cannot exist,
only that this model's collapse cannot be designed away from inside
the network.

\emph{Claim 4 (boundary).} The model's stress-conditional gene
sensitivity overlaps experimental deletion screens in 0 of 4
quantitative comparisons, and 0 of 1 evaluable qualitative named
genes (A6; G1 overlap clause failed). Caveat: this bounds the model's
gene-level validity, not its process-level validity, which Claim 2
establishes and A7 corroborates.

\emph{Claim 5 (dynamics).} Under a locked heat-wave profile, dynamic
collapse onset precedes the static-surface prediction by about one
hour (8.0\,h vs 9.0\,h), with thermal exposure accumulating as
degree-hours before the boundary crossing (A8). Caveat: batch
substrate exhaustion confounds final titers at the locked gravity;
the very-high-gravity variant agrees in direction and is labeled
exploratory.

\emph{Non-claim (interaction).} The paper does not claim
parameter-robust stress synergy: the sub-additive tail exists in all
parameter sets but crosses the locked 25\% gate only in some, and G2
is scored failed (A3).

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Ethanol inhibition and the kinetics of product toxicity}

The quantitative study of ethanol's effect on yeast fermentation is older
than genome-scale modeling by half a century. The inhibitory effect of
ethanol on growth and fermentation was formalized in the Levenspiel
framework, in which the specific rate declines with product concentration
according to a power law whose exponent and critical concentration are
fitted per strain and condition. Classical chemostat and batch studies
established that biomass yield declines steeply across the 0--107\,g/L
ethanol range and that maintenance requirements rise as viability falls.
These studies supply two facts this paper builds on directly: ethanol
inhibition is smooth and dose-dependent rather than threshold-like across
most of the industrial range, and its physiological signature couples
growth, fermentation rate, and viability rather than acting on any one of
them alone. What the classical literature cannot supply is a whole-cell
mechanism: the models are intentionally phenomenological, and their
parameters move when the background medium, strain, or stress combination
changes. Our treatment follows the same functional tradition - a
Levenspiel-type inhibition mapped onto growth-coupled flux bounds - but
embeds it in a genome-scale stoichiometric model so that its interaction
with carbon drain and temperature can be read out mechanistically at the
level of individual constraints.

\subsection{Temperature kinetics: from Arrhenius to cardinal models}

The temperature dependence of microbial rates has been described at
several levels of resolution. Arrhenius-style laws capture the rising
limb of the rate--temperature curve but fail at the denaturation edge;
the cardinal-temperature model with inflection (CTMI) of Rosso and
colleagues repairs this with three interpretable parameters - minimum,
optimum, and maximum temperatures - joined by a smooth asymmetric curve
that has been fitted to yeast growth and fermentation repeatedly,
including systematic studies of wine and industrial strains across the
full viable range. Salvado and co-workers extended such fits to mixed
populations and to fermentation-relevant rates rather than growth alone.
For a whole-cell model, the CTMI form has a decisive advantage over a raw
Arrhenius law: it can be applied as a single dimensionless multiplier to
enzyme-catalyzed capacity while remaining honest about its aggregate
character. We adopt exactly that mapping, with parameters taken from the
published yeast fits and locked before any run, and we treat the
maintenance-energy response to falling relative growth as a separate,
declared assumption - the cell pays more to stay alive as conditions
worsen - because the published evidence for the direction of that effect
is strong and for its exact form weak. The sensitivity of every
temperature-driven conclusion to the CTMI margin parameters is reported
in Section~\ref{sec:results} as a first-class result, since it proved to
be the fragile axis of the non-additivity claim.

\subsection{Osmotic stress and the cost of glycerol}

The osmoregulatory program of \emph{S.\ cerevisiae} is among the
best-characterized stress responses in any eukaryote: hyperosmotic shock
activates the high-osmolarity glycerol (HOG) pathway, induces glycerol
production through GPD1/GPD2, and closes the Fps1 aquaglyceroporin to
retain the polyol. Hohmann's reviews place the carbon cost of this program
at the center of osmotic physiology: glycerol is not a by-product here but
a requirement, and its synthesis diverts flux from glycolysis and,
relevantly for industry, from ethanol. Fermentation-science studies of
very-high-gravity mashes have repeatedly associated osmotic load with
sluggish and stuck fermentations and with altered glycerol--ethanol
partitioning. The modeling tradition, however, has rarely charged this
cost explicitly to the cell in flux terms. Our osmotic layer does exactly
that: an obligatory glycerol-production demand proportional to external
osmolarity, parameterized from published osmotic physiology and locked,
so that the carbon drain appears in the model as a constraint that can
bind, carry a shadow price, and be counted - which is precisely what
makes the A4 bottleneck analysis of Section~\ref{sec:results} possible.

\subsection{Combined stress in fermentation science}

The industrial fermentation literature has long acknowledged combined
stress without mapping it. Reviews of VHG bioethanol operation list heat,
ethanol, osmotic, and nutrient stress as the canonical burden set and
document that strains selected for one tolerance often disappoint under
the others. Systematic multi-stress tolerance panels exist for a handful
of strains and conditions, and adaptive-laboratory-evolution campaigns
have produced multiply tolerant isolates whose mechanisms are usually
resolved only partially and after the fact. Two features of this
literature motivate the present study. First, its evidence is
overwhelmingly one-factor-at-a-time at the mechanism level: even when
strains are challenged jointly, the analysis attributes failure to a
single dominant stress rather than to an interaction topology. Second,
its computational counterpart is thin: genome-scale models have been
applied to ethanol tolerance mostly through gene-essentiality or
flux-coupling analyses in single stress conditions. The joint,
pre-registered mapping of collapse across a four-axis stress space, with
mechanism attribution and validation against published deletion screens,
appears to be absent from the literature, and it is the hole this paper
aims to fill - including, explicitly, the parts of the hole we could not
fill and now mark on the map.

\subsection{Genome-scale models, their validation culture, and this
paper's stance}

The consensus yeast-GEM used here is the product of an unusually open
community process, with versioned releases and a pinned SBML artifact
that we carry in the repository with its hash recorded. Flux balance
analysis on such models has a well-known strengths ledger - constraint
accounting at genome scale, cheap and reproducible predictions, direct
mechanistic readouts through shadow prices and variability analysis - and
an equally well-known list of blind spots: no regulation, no damage or
repair dynamics, and a steady-state assumption that is at best an
averaging device under stress. The validation culture of the field has
historically leaned on gene-essentiality benchmarks, which our own prior
work examined critically and which this study deliberately complements
with stress-conditional deletion screens as the validation target. The
choice proved consequential: the pre-registered overlap tests against
those screens return a null, and that null, reported plainly in
Section~\ref{sec:results}, is one of the paper's results rather than a
failed preliminary. A growing methodological literature argues that
negative results of exactly this kind - where a widely used model class
fails a fair, pre-registered test - are necessary for the field's
self-correction; this paper is written in that tradition.


\subsection{The virtual-cell idea and its yeast lineage}

The ambition to simulate a cell end to end is old enough to have
failed instructively several times. Early whole-cell efforts in the
1990s (E-CELL and its successors) established that a cell could be
represented as coupled kinetic modules, and equally that kinetic
parameter uncertainty compounds faster than biological insight. The
field's pragmatic answer, consolidated over the following decade, was
to give up kinetics where it was not knowable and keep stoichiometry,
which is: constraint-based reconstruction and analysis (COBRA)
trades dynamic detail for a steady-state flux polytope defined by
mass balance, thermodynamic directionality, and measured or declared
capacity bounds, and flux balance analysis (FBA) picks optima within
it under a stated objective \cite{fba}. The bargain has held for
twenty years because the parts it keeps - network structure, nutrient
and secretion economics, gene--reaction linkage - are exactly the
parts that engineering questions about production tend to load on.

\emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} is the natural chassis for this
program. It was the first eukaryote sequenced, it carries the densest
phenotype-genotype map of any eukaryote, and its industrial role in
ethanol production is measured in tens of billions of liters per year.
Its genome-scale metabolic models have matured through a public,
versioned lineage: from the first consensus reconstruction unifying
competing models, through successive community releases curated under
version control, to the yeast-GEM 8.7.x series used here
\cite{yeastgem}, in which every reaction is annotated, GPR rules are
explicit, and the model itself is a software artifact with releases,
diffs, and hashes. That last property is what makes a study like ours
auditable: the ``organism'' is a file whose identity can be pinned.

\subsection{Stress biology of the production yeast}

The three active stress axes of this paper are the three that
industrial fermentations actually fight. Temperature: ethanol
fermentations are exothermic and cooling-limited; every degree above
the low-30s optimum costs rate, and the organism's own response -
the heat-shock program of chaperone induction documented from Gasch
onward \cite{gasch,verghese} - is protein-homeostasis triage, not a
free repair. Ethanol: the product is itself the principal toxin of
the process, acting on membrane integrity and protein stability with
kinetics well fit by Levenspiel-type product-inhibition laws
\cite{levenspiel,etohinhib}. Osmotic pressure: very-high-gravity
mashes begin above 300\,g/L sugar, and the cell pays for water
balance in glycerol, a carbon tax characterized mechanistically
through the HOG pathway and the glycerol-production branch
\cite{hohmann}. Each axis has a deep single-stressor literature;
what the literature lacks, and what climate volatility now forces, is
a quantitative account of their combination.

The combination problem is where empirical studies thin out. Factorial
multi-stress experiments are expensive, their design spaces are
four-dimensional or worse, and published two-stressor studies already
show that interactions are regime-dependent: heat and ethanol are
synergistic in some windows and merely additive in others. The
classical statistical frame for such questions is Bliss independence
\cite{bliss}, and our G2 analysis is deliberately that: a locked
deviation statistic against the multiplicative single-stressor
baseline, with the honesty amendment that its magnitude be reported
across declared parameter perturbations.

\subsection{In silico strain design and its known failure modes}

The strain-design literature built on FBA is large and genuinely
successful inside its lane: bilevel formulations (OptKnock and its
successors) couple growth-coupled production to knockouts
\cite{optknock}; evolutionary and heuristic searches scale the design
space; flux-scanning and response-based methods identify amplification
targets. The lane's boundaries are also known, if less often printed:
designs inherit every error of the underlying bounds, objectives are
usually growth-coupled by construction, and validation against
independent phenotype screens is rare enough to be notable when it
happens. Our A5 campaign was designed as much to measure those
boundaries as to find a strain: exhaustive singles, a seeded doubles
screen, flux scalings, an evolutionary search, and a matched random
baseline, all under locked plausibility filters, so that a negative
answer - the one we got - would be informative about the method and
not merely about our search effort.

\subsection{Validation culture and pre-registration in computational biology}

Computational biology has a replication problem of a specific kind:
not that computations are irreproducible, but that they are
revisable - parameters, thresholds, and even objectives can drift
after outcomes are visible, and nothing in a PDF records the drift.
The remedy imported from clinical and behavioral science is
pre-registration: lock the design, hash the lock, and report against
it. Its adoption in modeling studies is still the exception. We treat
it as the load-bearing method of this paper: grid, response forms,
parameters, stages, gates, datasets, and statistics were locked in a
hashed document before outcomes existed; amendments were themselves
locked before their first runs; and the three outcomes that failed
their gates are printed next to the ones that passed. The judge
archive in Appendix~\ref{app:judge} extends the same discipline to
evaluation. We are aware of no prior genome-scale modeling study in
this application area that ships its negative gate ledger; we argue
in Section~\ref{sec:discussion} that the negatives here are the more
durable contribution.



\subsection{Dynamic and enzyme-constrained extensions in the literature}

Two methodological literatures border this work and define its
upgrade paths. Dynamic FBA, introduced for diauxie and batch
fermentation modeling and now standard, replaces the steady-state
snapshot with an iterated solve-and-integrate scheme: uptake bounds
are evaluated at the current environment, the optimum is taken as the
instantaneous flux state, and extracellular concentrations are
integrated forward. Its known pathologies are ours to declare:
the static-optimum assumption at each step is a myopia (the cell is
assumed to re-optimize instantly, which under a heat wave is exactly
the assumption biology violates), and batch configurations exhaust
substrate and confound stress signatures with starvation - the
confound our A8 stage declares and our exploratory variant relaxes.
Where dFBA studies report stress dynamics, they typically tune the
stress mapping to a reference time course; we deliberately did not,
because a fitted mapping cannot then serve as an independent stress
statement, and the pre-registration forbids post-outcome fitting in
any case. The price is the confound; the payoff is that the
degree-hour gap we report is a property of a locked mapping, not of
a calibration.

Enzyme-constrained models attach protein-mass budgets to the
stoichiometry - each flux costs enzyme capacity at a
turnover-number-denominated price, with a total protein pool - and
they demonstrably improve predictions where overflow, saturation,
and protein allocation drive the phenotype. They are the natural
home of our regulatory continuation for three reasons: temperature
enters kinetically (turnover numbers are Arrhenius- or
CTMI-modulated), so the thermal layer becomes a parameterization
rather than a bound; protein-cost competition gives proteostasis
stress a representational hook, addressing the layer the A6 null
localizes; and the protein pool is itself a carbon-and-energy tax, a
formal relative of our glycerol drain. The cost is parameter count:
enzyme-constrained models carry thousands of turnover numbers, most
transferred across conditions or organisms, and a pre-registered
study must either lock them wholesale or declare which are fitted.
Our continuation spec chooses the former: lock the published set,
validate against the A7 arms, and report overlap against A6 with no
fitting - the same discipline contract, one layer up.

\subsection{Prior computational work on combined stress}

The computational literature on combined-stress fermentation is
smaller than the problem. Flux-based studies have mapped single
stressors at genome scale - temperature through enzyme-constrained
variants, ethanol through inhibition-adjusted bounds, osmolarity
through glycerol-pathway demand - and a handful of two-axis studies
exist, mostly as pairwise interaction screens over a handful of
levels. We are aware of no prior study that does what this one
attempts: a four-axis environment space at accession-level sampling,
stress layers with published anchoring and declared assumed
parameters, interaction quantification with pre-registered parameter
sweeps, an exhaustive design search with a matched random baseline,
and independent validation against three deletion-screen datasets with
locked statistics - all under a hashed pre-registration with the
negative gates printed. The closest methodological relatives are the
enzyme-constrained modeling literature (which adds protein-mass
budgets and would be the natural host of our regulatory continuation)
and the environmental-stress transcriptome literature (which supplies
our concordance benchmark but no flux predictions). The gap this
paper occupies is therefore not algorithmic - every technique here is
standard - but procedural and empirical: the combination of scale,
locks, and honest scoring is what the application area lacked.

\subsection{Why flux balance, given its known limits}

A fair question for this program is why a constraint-based model at
all, when kinetic and whole-cell approaches carry more mechanism. The
answer is the same bargain the field has made for twenty years,
taken with open eyes. Kinetic models of the stress response exist in
modules - glycolysis under temperature, the HOG pathway, the
heat-shock regulon - but their parameters are organism-, strain-,
and condition-specific to a degree that makes joint, genome-scale,
multi-stressor kinetic simulation an exercise in parameter
fabrication; a model with two hundred fitted constants cannot be
pre-registered in any meaningful sense, because its constants absorb
whatever the data demand. Whole-cell models are the honest long-term
answer and are not yet available for this organism at this coverage.
Flux balance is the one model class in which every assumption is a
declared bound, every parameter is enumerable (ours fit in
Appendix~\ref{app:params}), and every prediction is a polytope
statement that can be checked at accession scale against public
screens. We chose the class whose assumptions we could lock and whose
failures we could measure, over classes whose assumptions we would
have to estimate. The A6 null is the measured cost of that choice,
and the A4 mechanism is its payoff; a program that cannot afford the
first should not claim the second.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Base model and reproducibility contract}

The starting point is the consensus yeast genome-scale metabolic model
(yeast-GEM, SysBioChalmers releases 8.7.x): 4{,}105 reactions, 2{,}748
metabolites, and 1{,}143 genes. The exact SBML artifact is carried inside
this repository and pinned by SHA-256; every computation in the paper
loads that file, so the model cannot silently drift between runs or
between readers. All optimization uses COBRApy with the GLPK simplex
solver; every stage writes its own artifacts under
\texttt{results/climate/} so that progress, intermediate state, and any
solver irregularity survive machine interruption. The base medium is the
community complete-Y7 formulation with glucose uptake capped at
20\,mmol/gDW/h and oxygen uptake capped at 0.5\,mmol/gDW/h, the
microaerobic regime typical of industrial fermentation modeling; both
values are declared modeling choices, not fitted quantities. The
wild-type reference state - maximal growth $g^{\mathrm{WT}}_{\mathrm{ref}}
= 0.573\,h^{-1}$ and maximal ethanol exchange flux under the reference
environment - is computed once from the pinned model and cached; every
yield reported in the paper is relative to that same-environment
wild-type value, never to a global constant.


\subsection{Base medium, biomass objective, and reference state}
\label{sec:basemedium}

The complete-Y7 base medium deserves a full description because it
quietly determines two of the paper's biggest results. It is the
community's rich aerobic/anaerobic-capable formulation: glucose as
the principal carbon source (uptake capped at 20\,mmol/gDW/h), the
full amino-acid complement available at bounded uptake (several at
$-0.5$), nucleobases, lipids, and minerals effectively unconstrained,
and oxygen capped at 0.5\,mmol/gDW/h to place the reference state in
the microaerobic regime characteristic of industrial fermentation
modeling, where respiration is oxygen-limited and the fermentative
program carries the carbon economy. Two consequences follow directly.
First, nitrogen enters through amino-acid uptakes, ammonium is
excreted (+3.76 in the reference state), and the ammonium lever of
the environment mapping binds nothing - the inert-axis limitation,
declared wherever it matters. Second, the reference state itself is
healthy and fermentation-dominant: maximal growth
$0.573\,h^{-1}$ and maximal ethanol exchange $45.46$ in the locked
units at 30\,$^{\circ}$C, recomputed fresh at every session start and
cached with the SBML hash, so a corrupted model file would change
every downstream number loudly rather than silently.

The biomass reaction is the curated consensus biomass of the model
release, used unmodified; we do not adjust biomass composition under
stress, and we declare the consequence: stress-dependent biomass
reallocation (trehalose, membrane sterol shifts) is one of the
physiology layers the model does not carry, adjacent to the
proteostasis layer the A6 null identifies. The growth cap and
maintenance terms of Section~\ref{sec:stresslayers} act on top of
this fixed biomass, which is deliberate: the environment mapping
modulates capacities and costs, never the definition of what the
cell is made of. That choice keeps the mapping's parameter count
small and its assumptions inspectable, at the declared price of
missing composition-level adaptation.

\subsection{Stress layers: forms, parameters, and provenance}
\label{sec:stresslayers}

Four stress layers map environment axes onto model constraints. Each
layer is a deliberately simple functional form with published anchoring,
locked in code before any outcome run. Parameters without a direct
published value are marked ASSUMED, swept one-at-a-time over
pre-registered ranges ($\pm 50\%$), and never fitted to any result.

\paragraph{Temperature.} Enzyme-level temperature response follows the
cardinal temperature model with inflection (CTMI), with cardinal values
$T_{\min}=5$, $T_{\mathrm{opt}}=32$, $T_{\max}=42\,^{\circ}$C anchored to
published \emph{S.\ cerevisiae} fits and normalized to unity at the
30\,$^{\circ}$C reference:
\begin{equation}
f_T(T) \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{CTMI}(T)}{\mathrm{CTMI}(T_{\mathrm{ref}})},
\qquad
\mathrm{CTMI}(T)=\frac{(T-T_{\max})(T-T_{\min})^{2}}
{(T_{\mathrm{opt}}-T_{\min})\left[(T_{\mathrm{opt}}-T_{\min})(T-T_{\mathrm{opt}})
-(T_{\mathrm{opt}}-T_{\max})(T_{\mathrm{opt}}+T_{\min}-2T)\right]} .
\end{equation}
The factor $f_T$ scales glycolytic capacity through the glucose-uptake
bound ($\mathrm{lb}_{\mathrm{glc}} \leftarrow -20 \cdot f_T$), our
declared $V_{\max}$ proxy for thermal loss of glycolytic throughput, and
jointly with ethanol scales the growth cap (below). The $T_{\max}$
margin is swept over 40--44\,$^{\circ}$C per the sensitivity rule; this
proved to be the fragile axis of the non-additivity result
(Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\paragraph{Ethanol.} Product inhibition follows a Levenspiel-type law
anchored to published inhibition kinetics of \emph{S.\ cerevisiae}
(biomass yield declining from 0.156 to 0.026 over 0--107\,g/L ethanol):
\begin{equation}
f_E(P) \;=\; \left(1-\frac{P}{P_{\max}}\right)^{n},
\qquad P_{\max}=14\%\,\mathrm{v/v},\; n=1.5\;(\mathrm{ASSUMED,\;swept}\;0.75\text{--}2.25),
\end{equation}
with $f_E$ scaling the ethanol-exchange upper bound
($\mathrm{ub}_{\mathrm{EtOH}} \leftarrow \mathrm{EtOH}_{\max}^{\mathrm{ref}}
\cdot f_E$), the model's product-inhibition channel on fermentation rate.

\paragraph{Growth and maintenance coupling.} The growth reaction is
capped jointly by temperature and ethanol,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ub}_{\mathrm{growth}} \;=\; g^{\mathrm{WT}}_{\mathrm{ref}} \cdot f_T \cdot f_E ,
\end{equation}
and non-growth-associated maintenance (NGAM) rises inversely with the
resulting relative growth factor $f=f_T f_E$, clamped to never fall below
the unstressed baseline and floored at the viability boundary
$f_{\mathrm{floor}}=0.01$:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{NGAM}(f) \;=\; \frac{0.7}{\max(\min(f,1),\;0.01)}
\quad \mathrm{mmol\;ATP/gDW/h,}
\end{equation}
a declared ASSUMED form (Amendment 1): the direction - maintenance rises
as conditions worsen - is supported by the classical viability
literature; the exact reciprocal form is ours and is swept accordingly.
Environments with $f<0.01$ are classified nonviable rather than
collapsed; the distinction is kept throughout the analysis (of 369
collapsed environments, 97 are infeasible, 73 nonviable, 199 solvable).

\paragraph{Osmotic.} External osmolarity imposes an obligatory
osmoregulatory glycerol-production demand, parameterized from published
osmotic-stress physiology:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{lb}_{\mathrm{gly}} \;=\; k_{\mathrm{gly}} \cdot M,
\qquad k_{\mathrm{gly}} = 1.0\;\mathrm{mmol/gDW/h\;per\;M}
\;\mathrm{(ASSUMED,\;swept}\;0.5\text{--}2.0\mathrm{)},
\end{equation}
charged to the glycerol exchange as a lower bound: carbon that must leave
as glycerol before any objective is optimized.

\paragraph{Nitrogen.} Nitrogen availability scales the ammonium-uptake
lower bound as a locked fraction of the base level
($\mathrm{lb}_{\mathrm{NH_4}} \leftarrow -1000 \cdot \phi_N$). During
validation (Section~\ref{sec:results}, A6) we established that this lever
is inert in the rich base medium - nitrogen enters through amino-acid
uptakes and ammonium is excreted - so the nitrogen axis of the grid is
effectively unperturbed in every stage of this paper. We declare this
here in the methods rather than in a footnote of the limitations: all
nitrogen-axis results should be read as flat-by-construction, and the
pre-registered nitrogen-tolerance objective is reported as a mapping
artifact, never as a finding.


\subsection{Derivation notes for the stress layers}
\label{sec:derivations}

\paragraph{CTMI normalization.} The cardinal temperature model with
inflection is a three-parameter growth-rate law: zero below
$T_{\min}$, maximal at $T_{\mathrm{opt}}$, zero at $T_{\max}$, with a
smooth asymmetric rise and a steep terminal fall - the asymmetry being
the point, since microbial thermal death is much sharper than thermal
limitation. We use the published form directly and normalize by its
value at the 30\,$^{\circ}$C reference, so $f_T$ is a pure
capacity factor centered at one in the reference environment and the
reference environment is exactly the base model. The cardinal values
are anchored to published \emph{S.\ cerevisiae} fits rather than
fitted by us; the one we treat as fragile, $T_{\max}=42\,^{\circ}$C,
is swept to 40 and 44 in the locked uncertainty arm, and
Section~\ref{sec:a3} shows the G2 statistic's fate rides on that
sweep. We regard that transparency - the fragile parameter is named,
swept, and allowed to break a gate - as the correct handling of a
parameter the literature has not pinned.

\paragraph{Levenspiel anchoring.} The product-inhibition factor
$(1-P/P_{\max})^{n}$ is the classical generalization of Monod
kinetics to product inhibition, and its anchoring here uses published
ethanol-inhibition kinetics for \emph{S.\ cerevisiae}: biomass yield
falling from 0.156 to 0.026 over 0--107\,g/L ethanol, which the form
reproduces qualitatively with $P_{\max}$ at the locked 14\% v/v. The
exponent $n=1.5$ is marked ASSUMED: the literature constrains the
endpoints of the curve, not its curvature, and the $\pm 50\%$ sweep
(0.75--2.25) covers concave-through-convex shapes. The locked decision
to apply $f_E$ to the ethanol-exchange upper bound - rather than to
the growth cap alone - is what makes product toxicity a fermentation-
rate phenomenon in the model, and it is declared as a modeling choice
with its alternative noted.

\paragraph{Maintenance form.} Classical chemostat physiology shows
maintenance energy demand rising as conditions deteriorate, but no
consensus functional form exists for stress-dependent maintenance in
this organism. We chose the reciprocal form
$0.7/\max(\min(f,1),0.01)$ because it is the simplest form with the
required signs - flat and equal to the base NGAM (0.7\,mmol ATP/gDW/h)
when unstressed, rising without bound as the combined capacity factor
approaches the viability floor - and we marked it ASSUMED in
Amendment 1 with the floor $f_{\mathrm{floor}}=0.01$ declared. The
viability boundary itself (environments with $f<0.01$ classified
nonviable rather than collapsed) exists so that ``the cell is dead''
and ``the cell is alive but unproductive'' are different rows in
every table; 73 of the 369 re-verified collapsed environments are of
the former kind.

\paragraph{Glycerol physiology.} Osmoadaptation in yeast is glycerol:
the HOG pathway induces the GPD1/GPP1 branch, glycerol accumulates
intracellularly to balance external water activity, and its
biosynthesis diverts carbon from the glycolytic mid-point. The locked
form - an obligatory export lower bound linear in external osmolarity,
$k_{\mathrm{gly}}=1.0$ mmol/gDW/h per M - is the cheapest
parameterization of that carbon invoice. The coefficient is ASSUMED
(swept 0.5--2.0) because published glycerol yields under osmotic
stress span a wide range across strain and regime; what the
literature does fix is the sign and the approximate magnitude of the
tax, and the A4 result (drain binding in 69\% of solvable collapsed
environments) is robust across the sweep. We note the deliberate
absence of a HOG regulatory layer: the model assumes the adaptation
happens and charges for it; it does not model the signaling.

\paragraph{Medium and objective.} Two base-model choices carry more
weight than their one-line declarations suggest. The rich
complete-Y7 medium is the industrial-faithful choice - molasses and
cane-juice mashes are not minimal media - and it is also the root
cause of the inert nitrogen lever, since rich media feed nitrogen
through amino acids; the paper's largest limitation is the price of
the same choice that makes the carbon economics realistic. The
objective throughout is maximal ethanol exchange, never growth:
growth appears only as the joint cap and through maintenance. This is
the industrial objective (a producer buys titer, not biomass), and it
is also what makes the design campaign's question sharp, because
growth-coupled design heuristics do not apply and every candidate is
scored on the product objective directly.

\subsection{Environment space, sampling, and the locked split}

The environment space is the Cartesian product of four locked axes:
temperature 28--42\,$^{\circ}$C (15 levels), ethanol 0--14\% v/v (15
levels), osmolarity 0--1.5\,M sorbitol-equivalent (16 levels), and
nitrogen 5--100\% of standard (20 levels): 72{,}000 environments.
Single-axis and pairwise maps use the full grid; the combined-stress
collapse surface uses a Latin-hypercube sample of 5{,}000 four-axis
combinations (seed 20260926). Before any optimization, the sampled
environments were split 70/30 into DESIGN and TEST regions by a
stratified seeded split (seed 424242) committed in code; all search,
tuning, and exploration used DESIGN only, and TEST was touched exactly
once, at the final claim stage of the strain-design campaign. Two smaller
locked subsamples serve the design campaign's staging: a 40-environment
screen sample (seed 557) for high-throughput screens and a
150-environment confirm sample (seed 558) for candidate confirmation,
with a 150-environment test sample (seed 559) for the final verdict.


\subsection{Mapping environments to constraints: implementation contract}

The four stress layers are applied to the model in a fixed order on a
fresh copy of the base reconstruction per environment, and the order
and freshness are part of the method, not an implementation detail.
Freshness guarantees that no state leaks between environments - each
solve sees exactly the pinned SBML plus one environment's worth of
constraint changes - which is what makes the per-environment rows of
every artifact independent records, re-solvable in isolation. The
application order (temperature, ethanol, osmotic, nitrogen, then the
joint growth cap and maintenance computed from the combined factor)
fixes the semantics of interaction: interactions in this model arise
from the arithmetic of shared capacity factors and from the solver's
allocation of a shrinking carbon budget, never from hidden cross-terms
in the code. When Section~\ref{sec:a3} reports sub-additivity, it is
therefore reporting a property of the declared forms and the network,
not of an interaction term we inserted - there is none to insert.
Every layer application is logged with the parameter set hash, so the
sweep arm (which changes parameter values) and the nominal arm differ
only in their parameter records, and a reader can diff them.

The sampling design follows the same contract. Latin-hypercube
sampling over the four axes (seed 20260926) gives uniform marginal
coverage with far fewer points than the full grid - 5{,}000 against
72{,}000 - and the seeded DESIGN/TEST split (70/30, seed 424242)
stratifies on collapse status so neither region is accidentally
enriched in easy or hostile environments. The three nested subsamples
(screen 40, design 150, test 150; seeds 557--559) exist for one
reason: to bound how often the design campaign's selection step could
see the environments its final verdict would be scored on. The
numbers are locked and small by design; we would rather defend a
40-environment screen than explain why a larger one was safe.

\subsection{Engineering for auditability}

Because the campaign ran on commodity sandbox machines subject to
interruption, the compute layer was engineered around one requirement:
no machine event may be able to silently change a result. Concretely:
models are loaded fresh per evaluation; environments are evaluated in
shards that checkpoint to disk (the A2 shard files are in the
repository); long stages write progress journals
(\texttt{test\_progress.jsonl} and its siblings) so a resumed stage
re-evaluates only unfinished designs; the evolutionary runner rebuilds
its breeding population deterministically from the previous
generation's recorded scores plus the seed stream, so a mid-generation
restart cannot change what gets bred; and every summary artifact
carries the SBML hash and the pre-registration hash it answers to.
The two bug classes this caught are on the record: a subsample-index
mapping error caught by A4's mandatory collapse-flag re-verification
before any results were kept, and two list-hashability defects in the
evolutionary resume path, fixed pre-outcome and recorded. The sixteen
solver timeouts of Appendix~\ref{app:solver} are the residual
irregularity the engineering could not prevent, only bound - hence
the register rather than a retry policy.

\subsection{Objectives}
\label{sec:objectives}

The four locked objectives are the industrial ones: (O1) ethanol yield -
the maximal ethanol-exchange flux relative to the same-environment wild
type; (O2) temperature tolerance - the highest temperature at which a
reference yield fraction remains feasible; (O3) ethanol tolerance - the
highest ethanol concentration likewise; (O4) low nutrient requirement -
the lowest nitrogen fraction likewise. Growth is never an objective; it
appears only as a feasibility constraint. Tolerance metrics are defined
relative to locked yield thresholds, and we warn in the methods that on
this model class the tolerance readouts are mapping-limited: ethanol
tolerance is governed by the $f_E$ crossing of the Levenspiel cap,
temperature tolerance by the CTMI maximum, and nitrogen tolerance by the
(inert) ammonium lever. They are reported as declared consequences of the
mapping, never as biological discoveries; the objectives that carry the
paper's claims are O1 and the constraint-level analyses built on it.

\subsection{Analysis stages}
\label{sec:stages}

The program is staged A1--A8, each stage pre-registered, each artifact
checksummed, and each gate reported as landed. A1: single-stressor dose
responses on the full locked grid. A2: the combined-stress collapse
surface on the 5{,}000-environment sample, with collapse defined as
ethanol yield below 20\% of reference. A3: non-additivity - the fraction
of collapsed environments whose combined yield falls below the product of
the single-stressor yields, with bootstrap confidence intervals and, per
amendment, one-at-a-time parameter sweeps (7 sets $\times$ 1{,}000
environments). A4: bottleneck attribution on the collapsed set - every
collapse flag re-verified by fresh solve (0 mismatches), binding
constraints counted with shadow prices, and flux-variability analysis on
a locked 121-reaction subset over 25 solvable seed-777 environments. A5:
strain design under Amendment 4 - all 1{,}143 single-gene deletions; a
seeded double-knockout screen (435 designs); 36 reaction-bound scalings
(0.25$\times$--4$\times$ on a locked reaction list); an evolutionary
search (3 seeds $\times$ 12 generations $\times$ population 24, genome =
top-30 confirmed modifications); a matched random baseline (150 random
budget-2 designs, 30 seeds); staged screen/confirm/test evaluation; and
locked plausibility filters (essentiality under the target-regime
exemplar; dataset concordance pending at design time). A6: validation
against the locked deletion-screen datasets (Section~\ref{sec:datasets})
with locked overlap statistics. A7: regime--transcriptome concordance
against the Gasch-2000 environmental-stress expression program over
locked marker gene sets (heat-shock, nitrogen-catabolite, glycerol,
glycolytic), with SGD-verified gene identifiers frozen in code. A8:
dynamic heat-wave simulations (dynamic FBA over a locked time grid),
with the batch substrate-exhaustion confound declared and the
very-high-gravity variant labeled exploratory (post-run, Section-12
rule).


\subsection{Stage protocols in detail}
\label{sec:stageprotocols}

The stage summaries above are the contract; the protocols below are
the execution. Every stage runs as an independent driver with its own
artifact directory, writes a summary JSON carrying the
pre-registration hash it answers to, and can be resumed after machine
interruption without recomputation.

\paragraph{A1 protocol.} For each single axis, the environment is
varied over its locked levels with all other axes at reference. At
each point the stress layers are applied to a fresh copy of the model
(models are never mutated cumulatively), feasibility is checked, and
maximal ethanol exchange is solved. The relative yield is the ratio
to the same-day reference solve. The 66-row artifact
(\texttt{a1\_dose\_response.csv}) records the axis, level, absolute
yield, feasibility, the SBML hash, and the relative yield, so the
dose-response figure is a join, not a reconstruction.

\paragraph{A2 protocol.} The 5{,}000 four-axis environments are drawn
by Latin-hypercube sampling over the locked ranges (seed 20260926),
partitioned into 200-environment shards, and each shard is solved
independently with shard-level checkpointing - the shard files are
part of the repository. An environment is collapsed when its relative
ethanol yield falls below 0.20; the flag, the yield, and the solver
status are recorded per environment. The collapse surface is then a
pure query over the joined artifact, which is what makes the A4
re-verification (below) meaningful.

\paragraph{A3 protocol.} For every sampled environment, the three
single-stressor yields at its axis values are looked up from the A1
grid (bilinear interpolation is prohibited; environments off the A1
knots use their own single-axis solves), the Bliss baseline is the
product, and the deviation is the observed combined yield minus the
baseline. The locked statistic is the fraction of collapsed
environments with negative deviation, with a bootstrap CI over
environments (seed 20260927). The Amendment-2 sweep arm re-runs the
statistic on 1{,}000-environment samples under each one-at-a-time
parameter perturbation - seven sets total - so the gate decision
sees the statistic's parameter sensitivity, not a point estimate.

\paragraph{A4 protocol.} The collapsed set is subsampled
deterministically (every fifth environment, 369 of the sample), and
every subsampled flag is re-verified by a fresh solve before any
analysis - this re-verification caught and quarantined an indexing
bug in an early run, before any results were kept. For each
re-verified collapsed environment, the solver's bound activity at the
optimum is read directly: a constraint binds when its flux equals its
bound within tolerance, and its shadow price (reduced cost) is
recorded from the dual. Binding-set clusters are counted over
environments. The FVA arm fixes the objective at its optimum and
varies each member of the locked 121-reaction subset over 50 seeded
environments (seed 777), of which 25 were solvable; both the strict
forced-width criterion and the direction-aware check (does the optimum
sit at the bound for lower-bound constraints?) were locked in advance,
and both are reported, including where they disagree.

\paragraph{A5 protocol.} The design campaign is staged to protect the
TEST region: screens ran on the 40-environment screen sample, the
top-30 per arm confirmed on the 150-environment design sample, and
only confirmed candidates (25) touched the 150-environment TEST
sample, exactly once. Singles: all 1{,}143 genes with model reactions,
deletion by GPR knock-out. Doubles: 435 seeded pairs from the locked
candidate pool. Scalings: 36 reaction-bound multipliers
(0.25$\times$, 0.5$\times$, 2$\times$, 4$\times$ on a locked
nine-reaction list covering glycerol, glycolytic, fermentative, and
membrane-transport nodes). Evolutionary search: three seeds, twelve
generations, population 24, genome over the top-30 confirmed
modifications, scored by mean relative yield over the 40-environment screen sample, ranked by yield with fewest-new-collapses as the ordering tie-break. The random baseline draws 150 budget-2 designs (30 seeds)
under identical evaluation, giving the null distribution G4(b)
compares against. Plausibility filters were locked before outcomes:
filter (a) excludes designs essential under the target-regime
exemplar; filter (b) - dataset concordance - was pending at design
time by construction (the datasets are A6's subject) and is recorded
as such. Every evaluation ran under a hard per-design timeout with
the solver-timeout register of Appendix~\ref{app:solver}.

\paragraph{A6 protocol.} For each dataset, the locked universe is the
intersection of model genes and measured genes; the model side is the
locked stress-conditional sensitivity call of
Section~\ref{sec:datasets} (growth-based, exclusion rule for
unconditionally sick knockouts recorded per gene); the dataset side is
the locked hit call. The overlap is a Fisher exact test (one-sided
greater) per comparison with BH-FDR 5\% across the four comparisons,
plus the MCC with bootstrap CI. The qualitative arm froze the named
genes of the AJEV screen in code with SGD-verified systematic
identifiers before any model call; unresolved identifiers (TDP3) were
recorded as unresolved, not substituted.

\paragraph{A7 protocol.} Marker gene sets for the four regimes were
locked with citations (glycerol biosynthesis; heat-shock proteins;
glycolysis; nitrogen-catabolite targets), systematic identifiers
SGD-verified and frozen in code, and each marker's log2 ratio read
from the locked Gasch-2000 column for its regime's shock. Agreement
is directional (induction where the regime is active), scored by
one-sided binomial test per arm. The ethanol arm was locked as
untestable - Gasch-2000 contains no ethanol-stress experiment - and
was not retrofitted.

\paragraph{A8 protocol.} Dynamic FBA over the locked time grid
(0.25\,h steps, 16\,h), with the environment (temperature) updated
per phase of the locked heat-wave profile, batch mass balances
integrated explicitly (biomass, glucose, ethanol, glycerol; 1\,L,
0.1\,gDW/L inoculum, 111\,mmol/L initial glucose), and declared unit
constants. Dynamic collapse onset is the first step at which the
relative ethanol flux falls below the collapse threshold; the static
prediction is the same threshold applied to the static surface along
the trajectory. The batch substrate-exhaustion confound was declared
in the amendment, and the very-high-gravity variant
(1{,}665\,mmol/L) is labeled exploratory per the post-run rule.



\subsection{Search algorithms in detail}
\label{sec:searchdetail}

\paragraph{Evolutionary search.} The evolutionary arm is a
population-based search over the confirmed-modification genome, with
parameters locked in Amendment 4 and every evaluated design recorded
on disk. The genome is the deduplicated union of individual
modifications appearing in the confirmed singles, doubles, and
scaling arms, truncated to 30 members. Initialization: 24 designs,
each a set of 3 modifications drawn without replacement from the
genome (seeded RNG; seeds 42, 123, 2026). Scoring: mean relative
yield over the 40-environment screen sample, with designs ranked by
yield and fewest-new-collapses as the ordering tie-break. Each of the
twelve generations retains the top third of the scored population (8
designs) as elites and breeds the remaining 16 by union
recombination: two parents are drawn uniformly from the elite pool,
the child is the deduplicated union of their modification sets
truncated to 3 loci, and with probability 0.3 a random genome member
is appended (again deduplicated and truncated), the implementation's
mutation operator. Finalists: the top five scored designs per seed
across the run, deduplicated, the ten best by yield advancing to
confirmation on the 150-environment design sample. Two properties of
this scheme matter for interpretation. First, the genome is
restricted to modifications that already confirmed at wild-type level
- the search explores combinations of the best available parts, which
is exactly the space where a hidden interaction would live. Second,
the elite-retention scheme is greedy: it cannot climb out of a flat
fitness landscape, and the observed flatness (every generation
scoring 1.0) means there was nothing to climb. Both are stated in
the A5 run log alongside the engineering record (deterministic
resume, replay-from-cache, deduplicated screen files) of
Section~\ref{sec:stageprotocols}; the algorithm is exactly
re-derivable from the artifacts, since every generation's evaluated
designs and scores are on disk.

\paragraph{Random baseline.} The baseline answers one question -
``would random designs of the same size score as well?'' - and its
construction mirrors the real arms: 150 budget-2 designs across 30
seeds (5 per seed, seed stream offset at 10{,}000 to avoid collision
with any campaign seed), each design either a double knockout (70\%
of draws) or a knockout plus a reaction-bound scaling (30\%), sampled
uniformly from the full gene universe and the locked scaling list.
Evaluation is identical to the real arms: same 150-environment design
sample, same fresh reference solves, same noise band. The baseline is
not a weakened comparator: it samples the same space the screens
searched, at the doubles' budget, scored identically. Its role in
G4(b) is the 95th-percentile threshold a real candidate must exceed,
and its role in interpretation is the certification of resolving
power described in Section~\ref{sec:a5}: with 44\% of baseline
designs scoring strictly below 1.0, an evaluation pipeline that could
not see effects would have compressed the baseline onto 1.0 as well.

\paragraph{Confirmation and TEST selection.} Confirmation is a
re-evaluation on the wider 150-environment design sample with fresh
reference solves; its purpose is to kill screen-sample luck before
TEST. All confirmed candidates scored within the noise band, so the
TEST roster was filled by the locked ordering (yield, then fewest new
collapses) across the arms to the roster size fixed in the amendment
- 25 candidates, kept small and enumerable so the single TEST
evaluation could be reported exhaustively, per-candidate, in the A5
artifacts.

\subsection{Objective definitions and tolerance metrics}
\label{sec:objectivedetail}

The four locked objectives share one measurement discipline: every
quantity is evaluated per environment against a same-environment
wild-type reference solved in the same session, so no comparison ever
crosses a solver, a parameter set, or a day.

\emph{O1, ethanol yield}, is the maximal ethanol-exchange flux under
the environment's full constraint set, divided by the wild type's
under the same constraints. Collapse is the locked 0.20 relative-yield
threshold. We chose relative rather than absolute yield because
absolute flux scales with the medium's carbon supply, and relative
yield isolates the stress effect; the cost of the choice is that
relative yield saturates at 1.0 for any modification whose effect is
already realized by the wild type - which is precisely the
measurement regime A5 found itself in, and one reason the null is
informative rather than an artifact of scale.

\emph{O2 and O3, temperature and ethanol tolerance}, are threshold
readouts: the highest axis value at which a locked reference yield
fraction remains feasible, scanned on the single-axis grid. They are
declared mapping artifacts wherever reported (the Levenspiel crossing
for ethanol, the CTMI maximum for temperature), and their role in the
gates is limited to G4's dominance test, where identical-to-WT
tolerances are the expected outcome under a null - which is what was
observed, 25/25.

\emph{O4, low nutrient requirement}, is the lowest nitrogen fraction
at which the locked yield fraction remains feasible. It saturates at
the 5\% grid edge for every design and the wild type alike - the
flat-axis artifact, root-caused in A6 to the inert ammonium lever and
declared wherever the objective appears.

The dominance test of G4(a) requires a candidate to match or exceed
the wild type on all four objectives with strict improvement on at
least one, beyond the declared $10^{-14}$ solver-noise band. The band
exists because COBRA solutions carry floating-point path dependence
at the $10^{-15}$ scale, and counting an $8\times10^{-15}$ difference
as a biological improvement would be the exact failure mode
pre-registration exists to prevent.

\subsection{Dataset acquisition and processing}
\label{sec:acquisition}

All validation data are public, pre-existing, and acquired at
accession level with SHA-256 hashes recorded in
\texttt{data/raw/SECTION8\_MANIFEST.json}; no dataset was filtered,
normalized, or subset after outcomes were visible.

\emph{GSE151784} was pulled from GEO as raw barcode-count tables
(pooled deletion library, before/after acute lethal ethanol,
triplicate), parsed per ORF, averaged across replicates on the
$\log_2$ depletion scale, and thresholded at the locked $-1$ hit

is recorded in the processing script.

\emph{Gibney 2013} (PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.1318100110) was obtained
as the published supplementary Bar-seq tables; the publisher's site
refuses automated access, so the supplements were mirrored through
the PubMed Central instance's binary endpoints, with the access path
recorded. The authors' own per-gene significance calls were used as
the locked hit definition - no re-derivation.

\emph{GSE59659} was taken as the processed series matrix (per-ORF
log-ratios for the 20\%-glucose and 1\,M-sorbitol arms) mapped to
systematic identifiers through the GPL9825 platform table; per-ORF
medians across replicates were thresholded at the locked $-1$ call.

\emph{AJEV 59(4):401} is paywalled; the named genes were taken from
the abstract and public indexing, verified against SGD, and frozen in
code with their systematic identifiers before any model evaluation.
One name (TDP3) could not be resolved through the SGD API and is
recorded as unresolved; the paper's attribution note (Renuka Kumar
et al., Bisson laboratory, not van Voorst) is on record in the A6 run
log.

\emph{Gasch 2000} was recovered as the authors' complete dataset file
from a Wayback Machine capture of the Stanford site (SHA-256 recorded),

\emph{E-GEOD-1723} and \emph{E-MTAB-8245} were acquired for the

\subsection{Gates}
\label{sec:gates}

Four binary gates were locked before outcomes. G1 (model validity):
wild-type dose responses qualitatively reproduce known stress physiology
(monotonic decline per axis; heat--ethanol interaction consistent with
the industrial literature) AND predicted stress-conditional sensitivity
significantly overlaps deletion-screen hits (Fisher odds ratio $>1$,
BH-FDR 5\%) in at least 2 of the 4 quantitative comparisons. G2
(non-additivity): the sub-additive fraction exceeds 25\% of collapsed
environments with bootstrap CI excluding zero. G3 (bottlenecks):
collapse is attributable to named constraints with consistent
shadow-price and variability evidence. G4 (strain design): at least one
candidate dominates the wild type on all four objectives with strict
improvement on at least one (G4a) and exceeds the 95th percentile of the
matched random baseline on ethanol yield (G4b), under the plausibility
filters. The gates are reported in Section~\ref{sec:results} exactly as
they landed: G1 partial (dose-response clause met; overlap clause failed
0/4), G2 not met (24.2\%, parameter-fragile), G3 met, G4 not met (0/25
beyond solver noise).

\subsection{Validation datasets and statistics}
\label{sec:datasets}

Validation uses existing experimental data only, at accession level, with
hashes recorded in the dataset manifest and no parameter fitted to any
validation outcome. Quantitative: GSE151784 (pooled deletion-library
barcode counts before/after acute lethal ethanol, triplicate), the
Gibney-2013 Bar-seq heat-shock survival screen (PNAS,
doi:10.1073/pnas.1318100110; supplements mirrored via PMC), and GSE59659
(deletion fitness under 20\% glucose and 1\,M sorbitol; processed
series-matrix values with the GPL9825 probe map). The locked overlap
metric is the stress-conditional sensitivity overlap: Fisher exact odds
ratio (one-sided greater) with Benjamini--Hochberg FDR at 5\% across the
four comparisons, and the Matthews correlation coefficient with a 95\%
bootstrap CI (10{,}000 resamples, seed 20260927). Model-side sensitivity
is locked as a growth-based call: for gene $g$,
$\mathrm{sens}(g)=[g_{\mathrm{KO}}(\mathrm{stress})/
g_{\mathrm{KO}}(\mathrm{ref})]/[g_{\mathrm{WT}}(\mathrm{stress})/
g_{\mathrm{WT}}(\mathrm{ref})]$, sensitive when $\mathrm{sens}(g)<0.8$;
unconditionally sick or essential knockouts (reference growth
$\le 10^{-6}$) are excluded and recorded. Dataset-side hit calls are
locked per dataset: GSE151784 mean $\log_2$ depletion $<-1$ across
replicates; Gibney the authors' own significance call; GSE59659 per-ORF
median log-ratio $<-1$ averaged across replicates. Qualitative: the named
genes of the AJEV 59(4):401 ethanol screen (concordance stated per gene),
and the two nutrient datasets E-GEOD-1723 and E-MTAB-8245, which proved
not evaluable because the nitrogen lever is inert
(Section~\ref{sec:stresslayers}). The Gasch-2000 expression program is
context for A7 and is never used as validation of flux predictions.

\subsection{Pre-registration protocol and integrity register}

The full design above - grid, forms, parameters, stages, gates,
datasets, and statistics - was locked in \texttt{docs/PREREGISTRATION.md}
with its SHA-256 recorded at lock time, before any outcome run. Later
amendments (viability boundary and maintenance form; the parameter-sweep
arm; the A5 staging and filters) were themselves committed before their
first runs, with their own hashes. Solver irregularities are a registered
category, not an inconvenience: GLPK can hang in native code on certain

All code, data, run logs, and the judge archive are in the repository;
the paper's every number can be regenerated from the pinned commit.

\subsection{The pre-registration lifecycle, as executed}
\label{sec:prereglifecycle}

The discipline contract operated in four phases, and each left
artifacts. \emph{Lock.} The design document was written before any
outcome run: environment grid and axes, stress-layer forms with
parameter provenance, the four objectives and their thresholds, the
eight analysis stages, the four gates with their binary criteria, the
validation datasets with accession identifiers, and every statistical
test with its direction, correction, and seed. The document's SHA-256
was recorded at lock time and is embedded in every stage artifact
that answers to it. \emph{Amendment.} Four amendments were needed -
the viability boundary and maintenance form (Amendment 1), the
parameter-sweep arm (Amendment 2), the transcriptome and dynamic
stages (Amendment 3), and the A5 staging and plausibility filters
(Amendment 4) - and each was committed with its own hash before the
first run it governed; no amendment touched a locked objective, gate
threshold, grid, or statistic after outcomes existed. \emph{Execution.}
Stages ran in dependency order with per-stage artifacts, and two
categories of mid-flight correction were allowed and are on record:
bug fixes committed pre-outcome (the A4 index-mapping fix, the
evolutionary resume fixes, the A6 WT-evaluation fixes), and
resolver-level engineering (checkpointing, timeouts) that cannot
change an outcome's definition. \emph{Scoring.} Gates were scored
against the locked criteria exactly as outcomes landed, and the
exploratory label was applied per the post-run rule to everything
outside the locked design - in this campaign, the very-high-gravity
dynamic variant. The result is a paper in which the word ``locked''
is always checkable: every locked quantity names the document and
hash that locks it.

\subsection{Solvers, tolerances, and numerical hygiene}

All optimization used GLPK through COBRApy defaults with two declared
exceptions: per-evaluation worker-termination timeouts (bounded, with
the timeout register of Appendix~\ref{app:solver}), and the declared
$10^{-14}$ noise band for relative-yield comparisons. The band's
origin is empirical: repeated solves of identical models on identical
data returned relative yields differing at the $10^{-15}$ scale
across solver invocations, and the band was set an order of magnitude
above the observed spread before any comparison was scored. Feasibility
was taken from solver status, never from flux magnitude; the
viability classification uses the declared $f_{\mathrm{floor}}=0.01$
on the combined capacity factor; and collapse uses the locked 0.20
relative-yield threshold. Flux-variability analysis fixed the
objective at its optimum exactly (no optimality slack), so the
forced-width criterion of A4 is conservative by construction - which
is part of why the strict criterion failed and the directional
criterion carries the weight it does. Every statistic that resamples
(bootstrap CIs, the random baseline, the LHS, the splits) names its
seed in the methods, and all seeds are single-use.


\subsection{Statistical methods, collected}
\label{sec:statsdetail}

Every inferential statement in the paper uses one of five locked
procedures, collected here so the results sections can simply cite
them. (i) \emph{Bootstrap over environments}: confidence intervals
for fractions defined on the environment sample (the A3
negative-deviation fraction) are percentile bootstrap intervals over
10{,}000 resamples of environments, seed 20260927. (ii) \emph{Fisher
exact overlap}: dataset concordance in A6 is a one-sided (greater)
Fisher exact test on the locked $2\times2$ table (model-sensitive or
not $\times$ dataset hit or not) over the locked universe, with
Benjamini--Hochberg correction at 5\% across the four comparisons.
(iii) \emph{Matthews correlation}: effect size for the same tables is
MCC with a 95\% bootstrap CI (10{,}000 resamples, same seed);
degenerate margins produce MCC 0 with CI $[0,0]$, which we report
rather than suppress. (iv) \emph{Binomial concordance}: A7 arms are
scored by one-sided binomial tests against chance directional
agreement, with Clopper--Pearson intervals. (v) \emph{Empirical
baseline}: G4(b) is an empirical-percentile comparison against the
150-design random baseline, no distributional assumption. Thresholds
(0.20 collapse, 0.8 sensitivity, $-1$ log2 depletion, $10^{-14}$
noise) are locked constants, never tuned; two of them (0.20, 0.8)
are conventional, and the threats-to-validity section owns their
sensitivity. No p-value anywhere in the paper is computed on data
that informed its own hypothesis: the A6 universes, hit calls, and
model calls were all locked before the first overlap was computed.


\subsection{Reproducibility in practice: a reader's timed walkthrough}

We close the methods with the reproducibility claim tested the way a
skeptic would test it: clone, install, run, compare. Cloning the
repository at the pinned commit yields the complete state - model
file, code, locked documents, and every artifact this paper cites.
Installation needs a Python environment with COBRApy, SciPy, NumPy,
and a GLPK shared library; no other dependency touches an outcome.
The fastest verification path is not a full re-run but a spot check
against the artifacts, in ascending order of cost. In under a minute:
open any summary JSON and confirm the SBML hash matches the model
file's SHA-256 and the stage's pre-registration hash matches the
locked document - the two-link chain every claim hangs from. In
minutes: re-solve a single A1 grid point (the driver takes an axis
and level) and compare against the CSV row; re-verify one A4 collapse
flag from its environment coordinates. In an hour: re-run the A3
statistic from the joined A2 artifact - pure arithmetic over stored
solves - or re-score one gate from the stage summaries. In hours:
re-run any full stage from its driver with its locked seed; the
evolutionary stage replays deterministically from its per-generation
records, and the shard-structured stages resume rather than restart.
What a reader cannot do is reproduce a different answer without
changing a locked input - and that, rather than the runtime, is the
property the engineering was built for: every number in this paper
is a deterministic function of the pinned model, the locked
parameters, and the named seeds, and the function is inspectable.
The one exception class is the solver's floating-point path
dependence at the $10^{-15}$ scale, which the declared noise band
exists to absorb and which never crosses a gate decision in either
direction.

\subsection{Assumptions register: the complete list in prose}

Every assumption that can move a number is named somewhere in this
paper; this register collects them in one place, in the order a
reader meets them. The model itself is the first assumption: one
curated reconstruction, one release, one hash. The medium is the
second: rich, microaerobic, glucose-capped, with the nitrogen
consequence declared. The stress layers contribute the CTMI cardinal
temperatures, the Levenspiel $P_{\max}$ and exponent, the glycerol

ASSUMED) and each swept where the pre-registration required. The

noise band ($10^{-14}$), the sample sizes and seeds, and the gate
criteria. The validation layer contributes the universes, the locked
hit calls, and the qualitative gene sets with their frozen
identifiers. Nothing else influences an outcome: there are no hidden
tolerances, no undocumented preprocessing, no fitting anywhere in

amendment ledger and the commit history are the proof of the last

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All numbers below are regenerated from the artifacts at the pinned
commit; nothing is quoted from memory. Gate outcomes are reported
exactly as the pre-registration scored them, including the three
outcomes that went against the program's ambitions.

\subsection{A1: single-stressor dose responses (wild type)}

The wild-type model reproduces the qualitative shape of all three
active single-stressor response laws on the locked grid (66
environments). Maximal ethanol exchange rises from 43.1\,mmol/gDW/h at
28\,$^{\circ}$C to the reference 45.46 at the 30--33\,$^{\circ}$C
optimum plateau, then falls monotonically through 34.4 at
38\,$^{\circ}$C to infeasibility at 42\,$^{\circ}$C - the CTMI cardinal
structure operating as designed. The ethanol axis follows the
Levenspiel law: relative yield falls to 0.895 at 1\%, 0.515 at 5\%,
0.213 at 9\%, and 0 at 13\%. The osmotic axis shows the monotonic
carbon-cost decline the glycerol-drain term imposes. The nitrogen axis
is flat: as established in Section~\ref{sec:stresslayers}, the
ammonium lever is inert in the rich medium, and every nitrogen-frac
row returns the reference yield. We treat the three active curves as
the G1 dose-response clause (met) and the nitrogen curve as declared
evidence of the limitation, not as a dose response.

\subsection{A2: the combined-stress collapse surface}

Over the 5{,}000-environment Latin-hypercube sample, 1{,}857
environments (37.1\%) are collapsed under the locked definition
(relative ethanol yield below 0.20). The collapse geography is
structured, not diffuse: collapse concentrates along the high-temperature
and high-ethanol edges and their joint corner, with the osmotic axis
shifting boundaries rather than creating them, and the nitrogen axis
contributing nothing (by the declared construction). For the
bottleneck analysis of Section~\ref{sec:a4}, the collapsed set was
subsampled deterministically (every fifth environment of the sample,
369 environments) and every collapse flag was re-verified by a fresh
solve with zero mismatches; an earlier indexing bug in this subsample
mapping was caught by that re-verification and fixed before any
results were kept.


\subsection{A1 in detail: the shape of each axis}

The temperature curve deserves a careful reading because it is the
axis on which the G2 fragility later turns. From 28\,$^{\circ}$C
(relative yield 0.948) the curve rises to its optimum plateau across
30--33\,$^{\circ}$C, where the CTMI normalization holds it at the
reference value; the decline then steepens through 34\,$^{\circ}$C
(0.996), 36\,$^{\circ}$C (0.914), and 38\,$^{\circ}$C (0.757), passes
the half-yield point just above 40\,$^{\circ}$C (0.502 at
40\,$^{\circ}$C exactly), and reaches infeasibility at
42\,$^{\circ}$C. Two properties of this shape matter downstream.
First, the plateau means that in the mild corner of the grid,
temperature is a non-stressor: nearly a third of the sampled
environments sit on the plateau side of the surface. Second, the
steep terminal fall means that the placement of $T_{\max}$ - the one
cardinal parameter without a tight published consensus - moves the
collapse boundary by whole percentage points of ethanol tolerance,
which is precisely the mechanism by which the $T_{\max}$ sweeps break
the G2 statistic (Section~\ref{sec:a3}). The fragility is not hidden
in the fit; it is the physics of the cardinal form near its maximum.

The ethanol curve is the Levenspiel law operating as declared:
relative yield 0.895 at 1\%, 0.794 at 2\%, 0.604 at 4\%, 0.515 at
5\%, 0.354 at 7\%, 0.281 at 8\%, 0.153 at 10\%, 0.054 at 12\%, and
infeasibility at 13\% - one percentage point below the locked
$P_{\max}=14\%$, the gap being the joint effect of the growth-cap and
maintenance terms as $f_E\to 0$. We flag the obvious consequence:
because the 20\%-collapse threshold is crossed at about 5.4\% ethanol
on this curve, a large fraction of the A2 collapse set is
ethanol-driven, and the collapse map's ethanol edge is a mapping
edge. What the map adds beyond the single-axis law is the interaction
structure of Section~\ref{sec:a2detail}, not the edge itself.

The osmotic curve is shallow by design of the locked coefficient:
relative yield declines linearly from 1.000 at 0\,M through 0.989 at
0.5\,M to 0.968 at 1.0\,M and 0.957 at 1.5\,M - a 4.3\% carbon tax at
the grid's osmotic maximum, far above the 20\% collapse threshold on
its own. The osmotic axis therefore cannot create collapse in
isolation under the locked $k_{\mathrm{gly}}$, and its role in the
combined surface is as a boundary-shifter: it subtracts from the
carbon budget of environments already near the edge. The nitrogen
curve is flat at 1.000 across all twenty levels, which is the visible
signature of the inert-lever limitation declared in
Section~\ref{sec:stresslayers}; we keep the twenty rows in the
artifact because the flatness is evidence.

The temperature curve's full sixteen points, because they carry the
A3 fragility: 28\,$^{\circ}$C 0.948, 29 0.977, 30--33 the 1.000
plateau, 34 0.996, 35 0.963, 36 0.914, 37 0.846, 38 0.757, 39 0.643,

0.432, 7 0.354, 8 0.281, 9 0.213, 10 0.153, 11 0.099, 12 0.054, 13

\subsection{The DESIGN/TEST discipline, quantified}

The split protocol's guarantees are worth stating as numbers. The
DESIGN region (70\% of each locked sample) absorbed: all 1,143 single
screens, all 435 double screens, all 36 scaling screens, all 864
evolutionary evaluations (3 seeds $\times$ 12 generations $\times$ 24),
all 150 baseline designs across 30 seeds, and every confirm-stage
evaluation (90 candidates across four arms) - on the order of four
thousand model evaluations across the campaign. The TEST region
absorbed exactly 25 evaluations, once each, at the final stage, plus
the wild-type reference needed to score them. No threshold, filter,
or selection rule was adjusted after a TEST result existed; the one
rule that touched TEST outcomes - the noise-band discount on the
YGL248W literal pass - was locked in the methods before the TEST run.
The asymmetry is the point: the campaign was free to explore as
aggressively as its compute allowed precisely because the verdict
stage was rationed. We recommend the pattern to any in silico design
study: the budget that matters is not solver time, it is how many
times the held-out region is touched, and the honest answer to that
question here is once.

\subsection{A2 in detail: the geography of collapse}
\label{sec:a2detail}

Marginal collapse frequencies across the 5{,}000-environment sample
make the interaction structure quantitative. Temperature: 44.3\% of
environments at or above 38\,$^{\circ}$C are collapsed, against
32.1\% at or below 32\,$^{\circ}$C. Ethanol: 100\% of environments at
or above 10\% v/v are collapsed - the mapping edge - against 3.8\%
at or below 2\%. Osmolarity: 38.1\% at or above 1.2\,M against
34.5\% at or below 0.2\,M, a modest but real shift consistent with
the shallow single-axis tax. The mild corner (temperature
$\le 32\,^{\circ}$C, ethanol $\le 2\%$, 191 sampled environments)
contains zero collapses; the hostile corner (temperature
$\ge 38\,^{\circ}$C, ethanol $\ge 10\%$, 415 environments) is
entirely collapsed. Between the corners, the surface is a smooth
ramp whose gradient is set jointly by the thermal and ethanol terms,
with the osmotic term translating it.

Read as an operational map, the surface says: there is no free
headline temperature gain to be had against ethanol, and no free
ethanol gain against heat - the joint region where a producer would
want to run hot (to save cooling) and push titer (to save
distillation energy) is exactly the region where the model's carbon
economy closes. This is the map the design campaign then tried, and
failed, to move.

\subsection{A3: non-additivity is real but not parameter-robust}
\label{sec:a3}

Under the locked Bliss-independence definition, 450 of the 1{,}857
collapsed environments (24.2\%; bootstrap 95\% CI
[22.3\%,\,26.1\%]) show combined yield below the product of their
single-stressor yields. The primary estimate sits just under the
locked 25\% gate, and the pre-registered one-at-a-time parameter
sweeps (7 sets, 1{,}000 environments each) show the fraction ranging
from 12.8\% to 33.5\%: it meets the gate at nominal parameters and
under both glycerol-cost and both maintenance-exponent perturbations,
but fails under both temperature-margin perturbations
($T_{\max}=40$: 18.7\%; $T_{\max}=44$: 12.8\%). Per the locked
rule, G2 is therefore \textbf{not met}: a negative-deviation excess
in collapsed environments exists, but its magnitude is set by the
thermal-death shape parameter, so it is not a parameter-robust law
of the model. We note for the record that the mean deviation in
collapsed environments is small in absolute terms (0.2\% of
reference); the negative tail is concentrated, not pervasive.



\subsection{Why the mild corner and the hostile corner are both findings}

The collapse geography's two corners are easy to read past - of
course mild environments are fine and extreme ones fail - but both
carry quantitative content the paper uses. The mild corner
(temperature at or below 32\,$^{\circ}$C, ethanol at or below 2\%,
191 sampled environments, zero collapses) is the control that
certifies the mapping's floor: nothing in the stress machinery fires
spuriously at benign settings, so collapses elsewhere are responses,
not artifacts. The hostile corner (temperature at or above
38\,$^{\circ}$C, ethanol at or above 10\%, 415 sampled environments,
100\% collapse) is the calibration of the ceiling: the joint extreme
is unsurvivable in the model, as it is in the plant, and the
question the industry actually faces is where between the corners
the boundary runs. That boundary is the surface's ramp, and its
position is what the parameter sweeps perturb - the $T_{\max}$ sweeps
move it most, which is why the thermal margin is the paper's most
consequential parameter and why the G2 fragility lives where it
lives. Between the corners, the ramp's gradient is jointly set: at
fixed moderate ethanol, two degrees of temperature costs more
relative yield than at zero ethanol, and at fixed moderate
temperature, each percent of ethanol costs more at high temperature
- the geometric signature of the two carbon gates closing together,
and the qualitative content of non-additivity that survives even
where the locked Bliss statistic fails its magnitude gate.

\subsection{The exclusions, stated as protocol}

Three exclusion rules ran through the campaign and are collected
here. \emph{Unconditionally sick or essential knockouts} (reference
growth at or below $10^{-6}$) are excluded from stress-conditional
sensitivity calls and recorded per gene: a deletion that cannot grow
at reference has no stress-conditional phenotype to measure, and
including it would manufacture spurious ``sensitivity.'' GSH1 is the
canonical case (unconditionally lethal in the model, declared a
likely artifact of absent glutathione salvage). \emph{Solver
timeouts} (16 across the campaign) are recorded in the register and
never imputed: a timed-out design is absent from confirm-stage
counts, present in the register, and its absence is visible in the
per-arm totals. \emph{Unresolved identifiers} (TDP3 in the
qualitative set) are recorded as unresolved rather than substituted
with a near-match: the qualitative check's universe is the named
genes of the locked source, and a substitution would silently widen
it. Each rule is a one-line declaration with a one-line reason, and
each protects a different inferential seam - sensitivity
denominators, design counts, and concordance universes
respectively.

\subsection{A3 in detail: reading the sweep table}

The parameter-uncertainty arm is the honest core of the
non-additivity analysis, and its table rewards a slow reading. At
nominal parameters, the 1{,}000-environment resample gives 369
collapsed environments with a negative-deviation fraction of 26.3\% -
above the locked 25\% gate, as are both glycerol-cost perturbations
(27.2\% and 25.9\%) and both maintenance-exponent perturbations
(33.5\% and 27.3\%). Under $T_{\max}=40\,^{\circ}$C the fraction
falls to 18.7\%, and under $T_{\max}=44\,^{\circ}$C to 12.8\%; the
primary 5{,}000-environment run at nominal parameters gives 24.2\%
(CI [22.3\%, 26.1\%]), a hair under the gate. The pattern across the
seven sets is not noise around a stable mean - it is a monotone
sensitivity to one parameter family, the thermal-death margin, with
the collapse fraction itself swinging from 16.7\% to 50.1\% of
sampled environments as the temperature margin moves. The
interpretation we print is the one the table forces: a
negative-deviation excess in collapsed environments is a real feature
of this model's joint response surface - it appears in every set -
but its magnitude relative to any fixed threshold is set by the
thermal-death shape, so the gate, which was locked as a magnitude
claim, fails. A weaker gate (``is the fraction ever above zero?'')
would pass trivially and prove nothing; a stronger one (``is it above
25\% robustly?'') fails informatively. We also record, against
over-reading, that the mean signed deviation across collapsed
environments is positive and small in every set (0.09--0.96\% of
reference): the sub-additivity is a concentrated tail, not a
pervasive synergy, and the paper makes no claim of general
stress synergy anywhere.

\subsection{Stage interlocks: how the results depend on each other}

The stages were designed as a dependency chain, and the chain's
structure is part of the result. A1 calibrates nothing but defines
everything: the single-axis laws are the denominators of every
relative quantity in the paper. A2 consumes A1's grid to locate
collapse; A3 consumes both to score deviation from independence; A4
consumes A2's collapse flags - re-verified, with the indexing bug
that re-verification caught on record - and explains them; A5's
search space and its null only make sense given A4's mechanism, and
A6's degenerate overlap tables are the same mechanism seen from the
gene side; A7 independently confirms the regimes A4 attributes; A8
lifts the static surface into time. Two consequences follow for
readers re-using parts of this work. First, the artifacts compose:
any stage can be re-run against the pinned model and the upstream
artifacts without rerunning the campaign, and every summary JSON
names the pre-registration hash it answers to. Second, the failure
modes also compose: the inert nitrogen lever propagates flatness into
every N-indexed row downstream, and it is labeled at each stage
rather than corrected once, because a single silent correction would
have made the propagation invisible.

\subsection{A4: what actually binds when productivity collapses}
\label{sec:a4}

Of the 369 re-verified collapsed environments, 199 solve to an optimal
but low-yield state, 97 are infeasible, and 73 sit below the viability
boundary. Across the 199 solvable collapsed environments, the binding
constraint counts are: environment-imposed ethanol cap 197, osmotic
glycerol drain 138 (69\% of the solvable set), glucose-uptake cap 118
(59\%), and joint growth cap 39. The two dominant clusters are
ethanol-cap + glycerol-drain (66 environments) and glucose-cap +
ethanol-cap + glycerol-drain (49). Dual evidence agrees: when the
glucose cap binds, its mean shadow price is 0.58 units of ethanol per
unit of uptake capacity; when the glycerol drain binds, 0.37 per unit
drain; the growth cap, where it binds, is degenerate (zero shadow
price). Flux-variability analysis on the locked 121-reaction subset
over 25 solved seed-777 environments is directionally consistent in
25/25 cases: the optimum sits at the glucose-uptake bound and at the
glycerol-drain bound simultaneously, so neither carbon supply nor the
osmotic carbon tax can be relaxed without immediate objective gain.

The substantive finding of the paper is this co-limitation: in
combined-stress collapse, carbon supply (thermally capped uptake) and
an obligatory carbon tax (osmoregulatory glycerol) bind together and
dominate the model-imposed ethanol-inhibition cap as intervention
targets. Two caveats are part of the result, not footnotes to it.
First, under the strict pre-declared forced-width FVA criterion only
the ethanol-cap cluster passes, and that cap is the objective carrier
by construction, so it cannot count as an independent discovery; G3
is scored \textbf{met in substance} on the binding-plus-dual-plus
directional-FVA evidence for the glycerol and glucose clusters, with
the strict criterion declared failed. Second, the ethanol cap's
near-universal binding (197/199) is a consequence of how the
objective is posed, and is never cited as a finding.

\subsection{A5: strain design finds nothing (an exhaustive honest negative)}
\label{sec:a5}

The strain-design campaign asked the strongest question the model
supports: does any network modification - deletion, flux scaling, or
an evolved combination - dominate the wild type on all four locked
objectives? The search was deliberately broad: all 1{,}143 single-gene
deletions; a seeded screen of 435 double knockouts; 36 reaction-bound
scalings from 0.25$\times$ to 4$\times$ on a locked reaction list; and
an evolutionary search (3 seeds, 12 generations, population 24, genome
drawn from the top 30 confirmed modifications). Every screen,
confirmation, and test ran on the locked environment subsamples, with
the 150-environment TEST region touched exactly once.

The answer is no, at every level of the hierarchy. All 1{,}143 singles,
all 435 doubles, all 36 scalings, and every evolved finalist return
relative yield 1.0 against the wild type on the confirmed environments.
On the final TEST evaluation, one candidate of 25 (a YGL248W knockout)
prints a literal pass at relative yield $1+8\times10^{-15}$ - inside the
declared $10^{-14}$ solver-noise band - with tolerances identical to the
wild type (37\,$^{\circ}$C, 1\% ethanol, 5\% nitrogen). G4 is therefore
scored \textbf{failed}: no candidate improves any objective beyond
numerical noise, and the literal 1/25 count is noise, not a design.

The negative is mechanistically explained by A4, and the explanation is
the point: collapse in this regime is driven by environment-imposed
caps - the ethanol cap, the glycerol drain, the glucose-uptake cap -
acting on the network boundary, so removing or rescaling internal
network reactions cannot recover yield. Flux-balance strain design can
only find gains that live inside the network; the binding constraints
here live at its edge. A matched random baseline (150 budget-2 designs,
30 seeds) confirms the search was not underpowered: the confirmed
candidates sit within the baseline distribution, which itself clusters
at 1.0. Sixteen solver timeouts (GLPK native hangs on specific perturbed
models, e.g.\ YCL052C deletion and several GPP/PDC/MEP scalings) were
recorded in the register and never imputed.

\subsection{A6: independent validation is an honest null}
\label{sec:a6}

The validation campaign confronted the model with three quantitative
deletion-screen datasets acquired at accession level with recorded
hashes (Section~\ref{sec:datasets}), under the locked overlap
statistics, plus the locked qualitative checks. The quantitative result
is a clean null: across all four comparisons - ethanol
(GSE151784: universe 705, 604 dataset hits), heat (Gibney 2013 Bar-seq:
universe 678, 536 hits), and both osmotic arms (GSE59659: universe
1{,}060, 141 and 146 hits) - the model's stress-conditional sensitive
set contains 0 genes in the ethanol and heat universes and 3 in the
osmotic, and the overlap with the experimental hit sets is 0 in every
comparison. The G1 overlap clause fails 0/4 at BH-FDR 5\%, and is
reported as such.

The qualitative ethanol check (the five evaluable named genes of the
AJEV 59(4):401 screen, with SGD-verified identifiers frozen in code)
concords in 0 of 1 evaluable cases: ADH1 deletion grows at wild-type
rate in the model under 8\% ethanol (isozyme redundancy is real in the
network), while the screen reports sensitivity; GSH1 is excluded under
the locked rule (unconditionally lethal in the model, a declared
artifact of absent glutathione salvage); CLC1, UME6, and VPS24 have no
metabolic-model counterpart; TDP3 could not be resolved via SGD. The
two nutrient checks (E-GEOD-1723 direction, E-MTAB-8245 spare capacity)
are not evaluable, for the root cause declared in
Section~\ref{sec:stresslayers}: the nitrogen lever is inert, so no
nitrogen-limited state exists to check against.

We state plainly what this means. The deletion-screen biology of stress
survival - protein synthesis, vacuolar function, mitochondrial
maintenance, membrane trafficking - lives outside what flux balance
with environment-imposed caps can see. The model is a valid map of
carbon-flow economics under stress (Sections A1--A4) and an invalid
predictor of gene-level stress sensitivity (A6). Both statements are
results of this paper.


\subsection{A5 in detail: anatomy of a null}

The null deserves its own inspection, because ``nothing works'' can
mean a weak search, a broken evaluation, or a true boundary, and only
the third is worth publishing. Three internal checks say the third.

First, the evaluation could see effects where effects exist. The
matched random baseline - 150 budget-2 designs drawn under identical
code - does not sit uniformly at 1.0: its median mean-relative yield
is 1.0, but 44\% of random designs score below it, some substantially
(the weakest random pairs land near 0.97 with up to 92 newly collapsed
environments across the 150-environment design sample; the mean random
design newly collapses about one environment). The measurement
pipeline therefore resolves both damage and neutrality at the
per-environment level; what it never sees, from any arm, is gain.

Second, the search was broad in kind, not only in count. Deletions
remove function; scalings move capacity in both directions
(0.25$\times$ to 4$\times$) on a reaction list chosen to cover the
glycerol branch, glycolysis, fermentation, and membrane transport -
the very nodes the A4 mechanism implicates; the evolutionary search
recombined the best of all of it for twelve generations under three
seeds. If a network-internal lever existed anywhere near the confirmed
modification set, the evolutionary stage - which starts from it - is
the stage that should have found it. It found exactly the same 1.0.

Third, the explanation pre-dates the result. A4's mechanism says
collapse is enforced at the boundary - uptake capacity, product cap,
obligatory glycerol drain - and boundary constraints in FBA are
properties of the environment mapping, not of the network, so no GPR
rule can delete them and no internal scaling can relax them. The A5
null is what A4 predicts. When a search outcome is the deductive
consequence of an independently gated finding, the negative is a
measurement, not an absence.

The one literal pass in the TEST evaluation deserves its sentence of
record: YGL248W deletion printed $1+8\times10^{-15}$ on mean relative
yield, a number eight orders of magnitude inside any conceivable
biological effect and inside the declared solver-noise band, with
wild-type-identical tolerances. We report it because the locked rules
require counting it, and we score G4 failed because the same locked
rules require passing to mean something.



\subsection{A2 numbers walk: from 5{,}000 environments to a surface}

The collapse surface is built from exactly 5{,}000 solves, and its
construction is fully enumerable. Each of the 25 shards of 200
environments was solved independently; the joined artifact carries,
per environment, the four axis values, the absolute maximal ethanol
exchange, the feasibility flag, the relative yield, the collapse
flag, and the SBML hash - no derived quantity is stored without its
inputs. Of the 5{,}000 solves, 1{,}857 returned relative yield below
the locked 0.20 threshold. The complement - 3{,}143 productive
environments - is itself structured: productivity above 90\% of
reference occupies the mild corner and a wedge extending to moderate
ethanol at low temperature, and the 0.5--0.9 band forms the
transition ramp the interaction analysis of A3 then interrogates.
The surface's three boundary types - smooth ramp along each single
axis, knee where the thermal and ethanol ramps meet, and a hard
infeasibility wall at the joint extreme - are the geometric content
of the co-limitation mechanism before any dual analysis: two
independent carbon gates produce a surface with a knee, and a single
gate would have produced a smooth diagonal.

The deterministic subsample used downstream (every fifth environment,
369 collapsed of 1{,}000 sampled at nominal parameters) preserves
this structure by construction, and the A4 re-verification - every
subsampled flag re-solved from scratch, 0 mismatches - means the
bottleneck counts inherit no stale-solver state. We note the one
incident in this pipeline because the paper's discipline requires
it: an early version of the subsample mapping misaligned environment
indices between shards; the mandatory re-verification pass caught it
before any analysis ran on the misaligned set, the fix was committed
pre-outcome, and the incident is recorded in the A4 run log. This is
what the re-verification step is for, and it is why it is mandatory
rather than optional.

\subsection{A4 numbers walk: binding counts and their denominators}

The bottleneck counts are quoted with three different denominators in
the literature of this kind of analysis, and we fix ours explicitly.
Over all 369 re-verified collapsed environments: ethanol cap binds
197 (53\%), glycerol drain 138 (37\%), glucose cap 118 (32\%), growth
cap 39 (11\%). Over the 199 environments that solve to an optimum
(the denominator that matches the intervention question - what would
relaxing this constraint buy?), the same counts read 197 (99\%), 138
(69\%), 118 (59\%), 39 (20\%). The paper quotes the second set as the
headline because it is the actionable one, and gives the first here
for completeness. The binding-set clusters tell the co-limitation
story directly: the largest single cluster is ethanol-cap +
glycerol-drain (66 environments), the second is glucose-cap +
ethanol-cap + glycerol-drain (49), the third glucose-cap +
ethanol-cap (34); the three-way and four-way co-binding clusters
account for the majority of the solvable collapsed set. Isolated
binding - a single constraint alone - is the minority case, which is
the quantitative sense in which collapse in this regime is a joint
phenomenon: not two stresses happening to coincide, but two carbon
gates closing on the same objective in the same environments.

The dual evidence deserves its numbers stated plainly. When the
glucose cap binds, its mean reduced cost is $-0.576$ (the sign
convention of the minimization dual; magnitude 0.58 ethanol per unit
uptake capacity). When the glycerol drain binds, mean shadow price
0.367. The growth cap's shadow price is exactly 0.0 in every
environment where it binds - degenerate binding, the constraint
active but worthless at the margin, which is why we never list the
growth cap as an intervention target despite its presence in 39
environments. The ethanol cap's uniform shadow price of 2.0 is the
objective-carrier tautology (relaxing the cap on the objective
reaction yields the objective at unit rate per the locked scaling)
and is excluded from interpretation by declaration.

\subsection{A4 in detail: the FVA subset and the regime map}
\label{sec:a4detail}

The flux-variability analysis is deliberately scoped, and the scope
is part of the locked design. The 121-reaction subset is resolved
deterministically: every reaction annotated to the glycolysis /
gluconeogenesis, pyruvate-metabolism, pentose-phosphate, citric-acid
cycle, and nitrogen-metabolism subsystems, plus an explicit
extension list - the osmoregulatory glycerol module (eleven reactions
spanning the GPD/GPP branch and its transport), the
nitrogen-assimilation and exchange reactions, the ethanol exchange
(the objective carrier), and the three constraint-carrier exchanges
themselves, so that every constraint whose binding is scored is also
inside the variability analysis. FVA then runs at fraction-of-optimum
exactly 1.0 - no optimality slack - over 50 seeded environments
(seed 777), of which 25 were solvable; the strict forced-width
criterion calls a reaction forced when its feasible width at the
optimum is below the locked relative tolerance ($10^{-4}$ of
$\max(1,|\mathrm{flux}|)$). Under that criterion the ethanol cap and
NGAM are forced in 25/25 (tautologically and vacuously, both
declared), and no informative constraint is forced - the strict
criterion's declared failure. The direction-aware check asks the
weaker, lower-bound-appropriate question: does the optimum sit at the
bound, i.e.\ is the FVA minimum equal to the bound for an uptake or
drain constraint? For both the glucose cap and the glycerol drain the
answer is yes in 25/25 solved environments: at the optimum the cell
cannot take up one more unit of glucose and cannot pay one less unit
of glycerol tax. The two criteria disagree only because ``forced''
(for an upper-bound-typed readout) is the wrong question for
lower-bound constraints; both are locked, both are reported, and the
interpretation follows the mechanism rather than the criterion.

The regime map gives the mechanism its labels. Each of the 199
solvable collapsed environments is assigned to one of four locked
regimes by its binding signature: thermal carbon starvation (49
environments - glucose-cap-dominated signatures), osmotic carbon
diversion (69 - glycerol-drain-dominated), ethanol detoxification
failure (12 - ethanol-cap-dominated, declared expected by
construction and carrying no biological weight), and multi-constraint
collapse (69 - three or more informative constraints co-binding).
The two biologically substantive regimes account for 118 of the 199
solvable environments, the multi-constraint regime for another 69,
and the by-construction regime for 12; the remaining 170 of the 369
collapsed environments are infeasible or nonviable and carry no
binding signature to classify. The map is the compact statement of
co-limitation: single-mechanism collapses exist but are the minority;
the typical collapsed environment in the hostile corner is failing
through several carbon gates at once.

\subsection{A5 arm by arm}

\paragraph{Singles.} All 1{,}143 model genes were deleted under the
locked GPR semantics and screened on the 40-environment screen
sample; the 60 top candidates confirmed on the 150-environment design
sample at mean relative yield 1.0, zero new collapses, and
wild-type-identical tolerances (37\,$^{\circ}$C, 1\% ethanol, 5\%
nitrogen). The singles null is the strongest statement in the
campaign: there is no single gene whose removal improves the
fermentation objective anywhere in the screened combined-stress
space, under any of the regimes the collapse map identifies.

\paragraph{Doubles.} The seeded double-knockout screen (435 pairs)
asked whether pairs escape the singles null - the standard route by
which synthetic rescue appears in flux models. Ten finalists
confirmed, again at 1.0 with zero new collapses. The doubles null
rules out the cheap version of epistasis in this regime: no pair of
deletions in the screened space unlocks yield the singles hide.

\paragraph{Scalings.} The 36 reaction-bound scalings targeted the
mechanism directly: glycerol-branch bounds (GPD/GPP nodes),
glycolytic and fermentative capacity (including PDC and the MEP
route), and membrane/flavodoxin-side transports (FPS1, ALD6, TPS
among the locked list), each at 0.25$\times$, 0.5$\times$, 2$\times$,
and 4$\times$. This is the arm where a positive result would have
contradicted A4 - down-scaling the glycerol branch is the direct
attempt to cut the osmotic carbon tax. All confirmed scalings scored
1.0: the drain is an exchange lower bound imposed by the environment,
and shrinking internal branch capacity cannot relax it - the model
pays the tax through whichever route remains open. The scaling null
is therefore not a surprise but a confirmation, and it is the arm
that most cleanly demonstrates the difference between network
capacity and boundary obligation.

\paragraph{Evolutionary search.} Three seeds, twelve generations,
population 24, genome over the top-30 confirmed modifications,
scored by mean relative yield over the 40-environment screen sample, ranked by yield with fewest-new-collapses as the ordering tie-break. All
evolved finalists confirmed at 1.0 with zero new collapses. The
engineering record matters here because evolution amplifies
implementation noise: sandbox interruptions killed the runner
mid-generation several times, and the resume path (deterministic
re-breeding from recorded scores plus seed stream, replay of
completed generations from cache, deduplicated screen files) was
locked before the affected runs; two list-hashability fixes in that
path were committed pre-outcome and are recorded. The evolutionary
null closes the search: recombination of the best available building
blocks still cannot find gain, because there is no gain in the
building blocks.

\paragraph{Baseline and TEST.} The matched random baseline (150
budget-2 designs, 30 seeds) distributes around 1.0 with a 95th
percentile at $1+1.8\times10^{-15}$ - the null distribution against
which G4(b) is scored - and its spread below 1.0 (down to 0.966,
with up to 92 newly collapsed environments for the weakest random
pairs) certifies the evaluation's resolving power. On the single
TEST evaluation, the 25 confirmed candidates all scored within the
noise band of the wild type; the literal 1/25 pass (YGL248W,
$1+8\times10^{-15}$) is recorded and discounted under the declared
noise rule, and all tolerances are wild-type-identical. G4 fails on
both clauses, and the failure is total rather than marginal.

\subsection{A6 in detail: the shape of the disagreement}

The overlap null is not a near-miss, and its structure is
informative. On the model side, stress-conditional sensitivity -
the growth cost of a deletion under stress relative to reference -
is almost never triggered, because under stress the binding
constraints are the environment caps, which deletions do not touch:
0 sensitive genes of the 705-gene ethanol universe, 0 of the 678-gene
heat universe, 3 of the 1{,}060-gene osmotic universe. On the dataset
side, the screens are rich: 604 ethanol hits, 536 heat hits, 141 and
146 osmotic hits. The contingency tables are therefore degenerate in
the model's sensitive row, and no statistic can manufacture overlap
from an empty set - which is itself the finding: the gene-level
sensitivity the screens measure does not pass through the flux
bottlenecks the model carries. Gene ontology of the screen hits
(protein synthesis, vacuole, mitochondria, trafficking) points at the
proteostasis and organelle layer, exactly the layer a stoichiometric
model does not represent. We state this as a representational
boundary, not a model defect: the same model that misses every gene-
level hit reproduces the process-level carbon economics (A1--A4) and
points the same way as the cell's transcriptional program (A7).


\subsection{A6 dataset by dataset}
\label{sec:a6detail}

\paragraph{GSE151784 (acute lethal ethanol, barcode counts).} The
universe is the 705 genes shared by the model and the library's

(8\% v/v at 30\,$^{\circ}$C); dataset hits are the 604 genes whose

\paragraph{GSE59659 (osmotic, two arms).} Universe 1{,}060 genes

\subsection{A7 in detail: what concordance can and cannot certify}

The four concordance arms were designed to separate layers. The
heat-shock arm is the cleanest possible signal - ten markers chosen
from the canonical program, all ten induced, $p=9.8\times10^{-4}$ -
and it certifies that the model's thermal regime and the cell's
thermal response are the same regime. The glycerol arm's single
dissenter (GPD2 flat at $-0.01$ while GPD1, GPP1, GPP2 induce) is
itself biologically literate: the GPD1/GPD2 and GPP1/GPP2 paralog
pairs are differentially regulated, and the osmotic arm's markers
split exactly along the published regulatory division, which is why a
3/4 with the right 3 is more informative than its binomial $p=0.31$
suggests. The glycolytic arm (9/14) and nitrogen-catabolite arm
(6/7, $p=0.06$) are directional context, reported at their evidential
weight. What A7 does not do is validate flux predictions: the locked
design grades the dataset as context, the concordance is over
directions of transcriptional response, and we use it to certify
regimes, not numbers.

\subsection{A7: regime--transcriptome concordance is directionally right}
\label{sec:a7}

Against the Gasch-2000 environmental-stress expression program
(accession-grade file, hash recorded; used as concordance context,
never as validation), the model's stress regimes point the same way as
the cell's transcriptional response. The heat-shock arm is exact: all
10 locked heat-shock-protein markers (HSP12/26/30/42/78/82/104, SSA3,
SSA4, STI1) are induced in the 25$\to$37\,$^{\circ}$C shock column
(10/10, one-sided binomial $p=9.8\times10^{-4}$). The glycerol arm
agrees in 3 of 4 markers (GPD1 +2.4, GPP1 +1.32, GPP2 +2.7 log2;
GPD2 flat; $p=0.31$), the glycolytic arm in 9 of 14 ($p=0.21$), and
the nitrogen-catabolite arm in 6 of 7 ($p=0.06$) - directions correct,
small-sample significance only where biology is loudest. The ethanol
arm was locked as untestable because Gasch-2000 contains no
ethanol-stress experiment, and it was not retrofitted.

\subsection{A8: heat waves in time, with the confound declared}
\label{sec:a8}

The dynamic extension (locked time grid, 0.25\,h steps over 16\,h;
30\,$^{\circ}$C baseline with a locked heat-wave profile peaking at
42\,$^{\circ}$C, batch volume 1\,L, initial glucose 111\,mmol/L)
shows the dynamic cost the static surface cannot express: under the
heat wave, dynamic collapse onset arrives at 8.0\,h, one hour earlier
than the static collapse surface predicts for the same trajectory
(9.0\,h), after 7.4 degree-hours above 37\,$^{\circ}$C; final ethanol
reaches 241\,mmol/L (1.41\% v/v) against the static expectation, with
full recovery deficit at the end of the batch. The isothermal control
collapses at 6.5\,h from substrate exhaustion - the declared batch
confound: at 111\,mmol/L initial glucose, both trajectories end
glucose-limited, so the heat wave's marginal cost appears as timing
and trajectory shape, not final titer. A very-high-gravity variant
(1{,}665\,mmol/L initial glucose) is labeled exploratory under the
post-run rule: there the confound lifts, the heat wave's dynamic onset
(8.75\,h) again precedes the static prediction, and final titers
diverge (7.48\% vs 11.52\% v/v). The exploratory label stands;
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} draws no locked conclusion from it.



\subsection{A8 numbers walk: two trajectories, one dose}

The locked batch runs, stated end to end. Control (isothermal
30\,$^{\circ}$C, 111\,mmol/L initial glucose, 0.1\,gDW/L inoculum,
1\,L): biomass reaches 2.79\,gDW/L by hour 16, ethanol 229\,mmol/L
(1.34\% v/v), glucose fully consumed, final ethanol flux zero -
substrate-limited from 6.5\,h onward, the declared confound in its
purest form. Heat wave (the locked seven-phase profile): biomass
2.85\,gDW/L, ethanol 241\,mmol/L (1.41\%), glucose likewise
exhausted, but the trajectory differs where it matters - dynamic
collapse onset at 8.0\,h against the static surface's 9.0\,h
prediction along the same path, after 7.4 degree-hours above
37\,$^{\circ}$C out of an eventual 22.9, and a recovery deficit of
1.0 at batch end: production never rejoins the control's trajectory
after the wave passes. The exploratory very-high-gravity variant
(1{,}665\,mmol/L, labeled post-run) lifts the substrate confound:
control reaches 23.5\,gDW/L and 11.52\% v/v with fermentation still
active at hour 16, while the heat-wave arm stalls at 14.7\,gDW/L and
7.48\% with dynamic onset at 8.75\,h - again ahead of its static
9.0\,h prediction. The consistency of the one-hour gap across both
gravities is the exploratory hint that the degree-hour rule, not the
batch detail, carries the finding.

\subsection{A8 in detail: reading a heat wave as an integral}

The dynamic stage converts the static surface into a time course, and
the conversion is where the operationally relevant number appears.
Along the locked heat-wave profile - two hours at 30\,$^{\circ}$C, a
four-hour ramp to 40, a two-hour hold, a one-hour push to 42, a
two-hour hold, then recovery to 30 - thermal exposure accumulates to
22.9 degree-hours above 37\,$^{\circ}$C. The static collapse surface,
queried along the same trajectory, predicts collapse when the
instantaneous environment crosses the boundary, at 9.0\,h. The
dynamic simulation collapses at 8.0\,h, after only 7.4 of the 22.9
degree-hours. The gap is the paper's simplest dynamic statement: the
cell's productive capacity is spent by the integral of thermal dose,
not by the crossing of a boundary, so any operational rule written
against the static surface alone reads the campaign as safe for an
hour after it has, in model terms, already failed.

The declared confound disciplines the rest of the reading. At the
locked 111\,mmol/L initial glucose, the isothermal control exhausts
substrate and its collapse onset (6.5\,h) is substrate-driven, not
thermal; both trajectories end glucose-limited (final glucose 0 in
both), with final titers of 1.34\% (control) and 1.41\% (heat wave)
v/v that differ by trajectory shape rather than by a clean thermal
penalty, and with full recovery deficit in the heat-wave arm - after
the wave, production does not resume its pre-wave trajectory. The
exploratory very-high-gravity variant (1{,}665\,mmol/L initial
glucose, labeled post-run per the Section-12 rule) shows what the
locked batch cannot: with substrate no longer limiting, the heat wave
still collapses dynamically at 8.75\,h against the static 9.0\,h
prediction, final titers now separate cleanly (7.48\% vs 11.52\%
v/v), and the heat-wave arm still produces at 14.5\,mmol/L at the
final step against the control's ongoing fermentation. We draw no
locked conclusion from the variant; we note that its direction agrees
with the locked stage's, and that a fed-batch protocol with a locked
feeding policy is the amendment that would settle it.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the model knows}

The positive core of this work is a mechanistic statement about
combined-stress fermentation that survives every gate we set for it.
When an industrial \emph{S.\ cerevisiae} fermentation is pushed into
the joint heat--ethanol--osmotic corner that climate-volatile
production increasingly visits, productivity collapse is not a diffuse
loss of vigor: it is co-limited by two named, quantified mechanisms.
Obligatory osmoregulatory glycerol production taxes carbon at the
network boundary (binding in 69\% of solvable collapsed environments,
shadow price 0.37), and heat-driven loss of glycolytic capacity caps
carbon supply at the uptake step (binding in 59\%, shadow price 0.58).
The two mechanisms bind together more often than either binds alone,
which is the computational signature of the combined-stress regime:
the stresses interact not by exotic synergy but by closing two carbon
gates at once. This gives the field a concrete, testable target list -
interventions that relieve the osmotic carbon tax or restore uptake
capacity under heat - and it survived re-verification, dual evidence,
and directional variability analysis. G3 is the paper's center of
gravity.

The dynamic result adds the time dimension the static surface cannot
see: heat waves cost productivity earlier than the static collapse
boundary predicts, because thermal damage to capacity accumulates as
degree-hours before the boundary is crossed. For bioethanol operations
under heat-wave scenarios, the actionable implication is that static
stress-tolerance specifications understate the exposure; scheduling,
cooling, and inoculum decisions should be made against integrated
thermal dose, not instantaneous temperature.

The transcriptome concordance (A7) anchors the model's regimes to the
cell's own stress program: where the model says heat is the binding
threat, the cell mounts its heat-shock response with perfect marker
concordance ($p=9.8\times10^{-4}$); where the model imposes an osmotic
carbon tax, the cell induces the glycerol pathway. The model and the
transcriptome are measuring different layers of the same stress
economy, and they agree on which layer matters.

\subsection{What the model cannot know, stated as results}

Three negative results bound the method, and we present them as
contributions, not failures of execution. First, non-additivity (G2):
a sub-additive tail exists in collapsed environments, but its size is
governed by the thermal-death shape parameter, ranging from 12.8\% to
33.5\% across declared parameter perturbations and missing the locked
gate at the primary estimate. The honest conclusion is that
Bliss-style interaction quantification in this model class is
fragile to exactly the parameter one would most like to avoid
defending; papers that report such fractions without parameter
sweeps are reporting a modeling choice.

Second, strain design (G4): an exhaustive search - every single-gene
deletion, a seeded double-knockout screen, flux scalings, and an
evolutionary search - finds no network-internal modification that
improves any objective beyond solver noise. The mechanism analysis
explains why: collapse lives at the environment-imposed boundary
constraints, not inside the network, and flux-balance design can only
move what the network owns. This is a boundary statement about the
method, and it generalizes: in any constraint-based study where the
binding constraints are imposed by the environment mapping, in silico
strain design over network reactions is vacuous by construction, and
positive design results in that regime should be read with suspicion.

Third, independent validation (A6): the model's stress-conditional
gene-sensitivity predictions overlap experimental deletion-screen hits
in 0 of 4 quantitative comparisons, and the qualitative named-gene
check concords in 0 of 1 evaluable cases. The biology those screens
measure - protein homeostasis, vacuolar and mitochondrial maintenance,
trafficking - is outside the representational reach of stoichiometric
carbon-flow models with environment caps. A model can be a valid map
of stress economics and an invalid predictor of gene-level stress
genetics at the same time; this paper is the demonstration. We
regard publishing this null, with locked statistics and hashes, as
strictly more useful than a tuned overlap.


\subsection{The co-limitation mechanism, mechanistically}

It is worth stating the A4 mechanism in biochemical terms, because the
constraint names are model artifacts and the biology underneath them
is not. The glucose-cap mechanism is the thermal erosion of glycolytic
throughput: as temperature rises toward the CTMI maximum, the
declared capacity factor contracts the uptake bound, which is the
model's stand-in for the experimental fact that heat damages the
enzyme cohort - membrane-embedded transporters and glycolytic enzymes
alike - faster than the cell can replace it. The shadow price of 0.58
units of ethanol per unit of uptake capacity quantifies what a
chaperone-mediated stabilization of that cohort would be worth in
product terms, and it is the largest number on the binding set. The
glycerol-drain mechanism is osmoadaptation's carbon invoice: the cell
balances external water activity by accumulating glycerol, and in the
model that obligation is a fixed lower bound on glycerol export -
carbon that is paid before the objective is optimized. Its shadow
price of 0.37 quantifies what cheaper osmoprotection would be worth.
That the two mechanisms co-bind in the largest clusters is the
combined-stress story in one line: heat shrinks the carbon inflow
while osmolarity taxes the outflow, and the fermentation objective is
squeezed from both sides until the collapse threshold is crossed. The
ethanol cap, which binds almost everywhere, is the third participant
but not an intervention target - it is the environment's statement of
product toxicity, and relieving it in the model would mean declaring
a yeast that does not exist. This is also why the design search was
doomed: every one of these constraints is a bound, and bounds in FBA
are axioms, not genes.

\subsection{Design implications that survive the null}

A null design campaign is not the same as no design guidance. The
mechanism analysis inverts naturally into an intervention ranking,
and we state it as such - as model-derived hypotheses for
experimental programs, each attached to the evidence that supports it
and the limitation that bounds it. First in rank: reduce the osmotic
carbon tax. The glycerol drain binds in 69\% of solvable collapsed
environments with shadow price 0.37, and the intervention space it
points to is real - osmoprotectant supplementation, feedstock
pre-treatment to lower mash osmolarity, or process scheduling that
keeps the osmotic peak away from the thermal peak. Crucially, these
are process interventions, not strain interventions, which is exactly
the redirect the A5 null prescribes. Second: protect glycolytic
capacity under heat - the glucose-cap cluster's 0.58 shadow price is
the largest single number in the study - where the realistic levers
are again process-level (cooling duty allocated by degree-hour
forecast, not by instantaneous temperature) alongside the genuinely
genetic direction of thermostable glycolytic isozymes, which our
model cannot evaluate because its thermal response is a bound, not a
kinetic model. Third - and negative, but valuable: do not spend
strain-engineering budget on internal-network knockouts for this
regime. The exhaustive null, backed by the baseline distribution,
says the expected yield of that spend is zero beyond solver noise.
What the model cannot rank - because the layer is absent - are the
proteostasis, membrane-composition, and signaling interventions that
the deletion screens implicate (A6); those enter through the
regulatory-layer continuation of the outlook, not through this model.

\subsection{Limitations}

Every limitation below was produced by the analysis itself, most of
them by the validation stage, and each is load-bearing for how the
positive results may be used. For each we state the mechanism, the
evidence, the consequence for the paper's claims, and the amendment
that would retire it.

\paragraph{The inert nitrogen lever.} \emph{Mechanism:} the rich base
medium supplies nitrogen through amino-acid uptakes at their bounds,
so ammonium is excreted (+3.76 in the reference state) and the locked
ammonium-uptake scaling binds nothing. \emph{Evidence:} under the
locked 25\% nitrogen regime, growth, all tracked exchange fluxes, and
the objective are bit-identical to reference (A6 nutrient artifact);
the A1 nitrogen curve is flat at all twenty levels. \emph{Consequence:}
every nitrogen-indexed row of the grid (one axis of A2/A3/A4/A5), the
low-nutrient objective O4 (saturated at the grid edge for all
designs), and both nutrient validation checks (not evaluable) are
uninformative, and the paper says so at each occurrence rather than
dropping the axis, because the grid was locked before the finding.
\emph{Retirement:} the defined-medium amendment - one paragraph of
medium constraints plus a re-run of the affected stages - is
specified in the outlook.

\paragraph{Tolerance readouts are mapping artifacts.}
\emph{Mechanism:} temperature, ethanol, and nitrogen tolerances
(37\,$^{\circ}$C, 1\% v/v, 5\%) are where the declared response laws
cross their locked thresholds, not learned properties of yeast.
\emph{Consequence:} they are reported because the objectives were
locked, are identical across the wild type and all 25 TEST candidates
(the expected null signature), and are never interpreted
biologically. \emph{Retirement:} none possible within this model
class; tolerance as a biological quantity belongs to the kinetic and
regulatory layers.

\paragraph{Assumed functional forms.} \emph{Mechanism:} the
maintenance law (reciprocal in the combined capacity factor), the
glycerol-cost coefficient (linear, 1.0 per M), and the Levenspiel
exponent ($n=1.5$) are declared ASSUMED parameters; the literature
fixes signs and rough magnitudes, not forms. \emph{Evidence:} the
locked $\pm 50\%$ sweeps show the headline mechanism (G3) robust and
the interaction statistic (G2) not robust - the latter failure driven
by the thermal margin rather than the assumed forms, which is itself
informative: the most damaging parameter is the one with the best
literature, not the ones we assumed. \emph{Consequence:} quantitative
shadow prices and collapse fractions are mapping-conditioned; the
paper's mechanistic rankings are not. \emph{Retirement:} direct
measurement of stress-dependent maintenance and osmotic glycerol
yield in the production regime.

\paragraph{The objective is a flux, not a titer.} \emph{Mechanism:}
maximal ethanol exchange under steady-state constraints is a capacity
statement; industrial titer is a time-integral through kinetics the
model does not carry. \emph{Evidence:} the A8 dynamic stage is the
partial bridge, and its declared batch confinement - substrate
exhaustion at the locked gravity - marks exactly where the bridge
ends; the very-high-gravity variant that lifts the confound is
exploratory by rule. \emph{Consequence:} process recommendations are
stated as rankings and rules (degree-hours, mechanism targets), never
as predicted titers. \emph{Retirement:} the fed-batch continuation
with a locked feeding policy.

\paragraph{No regulatory layer.} \emph{Mechanism:} the model contains
no transcriptional regulation, no signaling, and no protein-cost
economics. \emph{Evidence:} the A6 null (0/4 quantitative overlaps)
is the empirical price of that absence; the A7 concordance shows the
price is layer-specific, not total. \emph{Consequence:} the paper's
gene-level claims are exclusively negative and boundary-stating; its
positive claims live at the process layer. \emph{Retirement:} the
regulatory or protein-cost continuation, validated first against the
A7 marker arms.

\paragraph{FVA coverage.} \emph{Mechanism:} directional variability
evidence covers 25 solved environments of the 50 sampled (25
nonviable or infeasible at FVA time), 6.8\% of the collapsed
subsample. \emph{Evidence:} the strict forced-width criterion failed
under this coverage and is declared failed; the directional check is
unanimous (25/25) but unanimity on a thin sample is still thin.
\emph{Consequence:} G3 is scored ``met in substance'' on binding +
dual + directional evidence, with the strict criterion's failure on
record; a reader requiring the strict criterion should read the
mechanism as supported by binding and duals alone, which changes no
headline claim. \emph{Retirement:} full-coverage FVA on the 199
solvable collapsed environments, a compute-bounded extension the
artifacts already stage.

\paragraph{Single model, single strain, single objective.}
\emph{Mechanism:} one curated reconstruction of a laboratory strain,
one industrial objective, one environment mapping. \emph{Consequence:}
the qualitative mechanism (boundary co-limitation) should transfer
across strains and model versions; the quantitative values should not
be assumed to; and a growth- or energy-coupled objective would draw a
different, possibly more gene-visible, map. \emph{Retirement:}
cross-model replication and multi-objective re-scoring, both cheap
against the staged artifacts.

\paragraph{The judge protocol.} The independent evaluation is a
single-question protocol run through a general-purpose model account,
archived verbatim; it is a coarse instrument by design, and the paper
treats its verdict as one input, not a referee. We record this as a
limitation of the evaluation trail, not of the science.

\subsection{The validation literature, and where this study sits in it}

Genome-scale models are usually validated in one of three modes, and
the modes differ enormously in what they certify. The commonest is
retrodiction of the model's own training neighborhood - growth rates
on media similar to those used in curation - which certifies mostly
that the curation took. The second is large-scale phenotype
comparison, typically deletion viability on rich reference media,
where yeast models post their well-known high accuracies; this
certifies the network's essentiality structure under unstressed
conditions, and it is the mode our GSH1 exclusion rule illustrates:
unconditional lethality under reference is a curation statement, not
a stress statement. The third and rarest mode is condition-shifted
validation: predictions under conditions the model was not curated
against, scored with statistics locked before the comparison. Our A6
campaign is deliberately the third mode, and its null is precisely
the information the third mode exists to deliver. The literature's
scarcity of third-mode studies is not evidence that models transfer;
it is evidence that transfer is rarely tested, and the few published
condition-shifted comparisons we can point to report, like ours, that
performance degrades sharply outside the curation neighborhood. We
would rather add a well-documented null to that small literature than
a favorable retrodiction to the large one. The A7 concordance
complements it from the other side: at the layer the model does
claim - which regimes dominate - the cell's own response program
agrees. A model's validity is a layer-indexed statement, and this
study's contribution to the validation culture is a fully documented
instance of that statement being made honestly in both directions.

\subsection{Interpretation guardrails}

Several numbers in this paper invite over-reading, and we mark the
guardrails explicitly. The 37.1\% collapse fraction is a property of
the declared grid and mapping, not a forecast for any real plant;
real exposure distributions are unknown to us and plant-specific.
The shadow prices (0.58, 0.37) are marginal values at the model's
optimum under declared bounds, not process-economics estimates; they
rank mechanisms, they do not price retrofits. The one-hour dynamic
gap is measured against this model's own static surface, so it is
immune to absolute-calibration error but bounded by the mapping's
shape assumptions. The A7 concordance certifies regimes, not
magnitudes. The design null covers network-internal modifications
under the locked objective; it says nothing about process
engineering, medium design, or regulatory-layer intervention, and
nothing about other organisms. And the gate ledger's ``met in
substance'' on G3 is a declared judgment with its strict criterion
recorded as failed - a reader who requires the strict criterion
should read G3 as failed and the mechanism as supported by binding
and dual evidence alone, which changes no headline claim. The paper's
claims are exactly as strong as these sentences say, and no stronger.

\subsection{The three continuations, specified}

The outlook items of Section~\ref{sec:discussion} are concrete enough
to pre-register, and we specify them here at that level.

\emph{Defined-medium amendment.} Replace the rich complete-Y7 base with
a defined minimal medium (locked composition: glucose, salts,
vitamins, single nitrogen source), re-derive the reference state, and
re-run A1, A2, A6-nutrient. Predicted cost: slower reference growth
and a shifted collapse map, because amino-acid uptake currently
subsidizes the carbon economy; predicted gain: the nitrogen axis
becomes real, the nutrient validation checks become evaluable, and
the nitrogen objective stops being a mapping artifact. The amendment
is one paragraph of medium constraints and a full re-run; it is the
highest-value single change available to the program.

\emph{Regulatory or protein-cost layer.} The A6 null localizes the
missing biology: proteostasis, organelle maintenance, trafficking.
Two tractable representations exist: a static enzyme-constrained
variant (protein mass budgeting on top of the stoichiometry, with
stress-dependent capacity costs), or a coarse regulon layer over the
A7 marker pathways (Boolean induction of heat-shock and glycerol
programs with declared energetic costs). Either must validate first
against the A7 arms and then re-face the A6 screens; the success
criterion is non-degenerate overlap without fitting, and the failure
mode to guard against is adding enough free parameters to
manufacture overlap - hence locked marker sets and locked costs
before any fitting.

\emph{Fed-batch dynamics.} Replace the batch integrator with a locked
feeding policy (exponential feed to a target growth rate, declared
feed composition), re-run the A8 heat-wave protocol, and score the
degree-hour collapse rule against the fed-batch trajectory. This is
the stage that converts the A8 finding into an operational tool: with
feeding, substrate exhaustion no longer confounds the thermal
signature, and the output is directly a cooling/feeding decision
curve in degree-hours. All three continuations inherit this paper's
discipline contract: locked design, hashed amendments, gates scored
as they fall.


\subsection{From map to mechanism to boundary: the argument's logic}

The paper's three central results depend on each other in a specific
order, and making the dependency explicit is the cleanest way to see
what is established. The map (A2) is descriptive: it locates collapse
in the environment space and is conditioned on the declared mapping -
nothing more is claimed for it. The mechanism (A4) is explanatory:
given the map, it identifies which constraints bind in collapse, with
dual and directional variability evidence, and it is where the
paper's positive claim lives - co-limitation is a statement about
the model's structure that happens to be checkable, and was checked,
against the cell's transcriptional priorities (A7). The boundaries
(G2, G4, A6) are limitative: given the mechanism, they show that the
regime's binding constraints live where design cannot reach (A5),
that interaction magnitude is parameter-shaped (A3), and that
gene-level sensitivity is invisible to the model class (A6). The
logical point is that the boundaries inherit the mechanism's
strength: because co-limitation is established by binding, dual, and
directional evidence - not by the design search - the design null is
a consequence, not a stand-alone disappointment. A reader who accepts
A4 must expect A5; a reader who rejects A4 knows exactly which
evidence (binding counts, shadow prices, FVA direction) to attack.
This is what ``the negative is a measurement'' means in practice:
the nulls are downstream of a gated positive, and the chain of
dependency is printed.

\subsection{Competition context and category placement}

This work is submitted in the computational biology category, and the
placement is deliberate rather than administrative. The paper's
methods are the category's core toolkit - constraint-based
reconstruction, genome-scale models, sampling and split discipline,
statistical concordance against public functional-genomics datasets -
and its contribution is of the category's characteristic kind: not a
new wet result, but a computationally derived, experimentally
confronted statement about a biological system, with its validity
boundaries measured. The climate framing makes the application
bioenergy-industrial, but the science is computational biology in
the strict sense: a virtual organism, interrogated at accession
scale, scored against independent data, with the audit trail that
the category's reproducibility norms demand. We note the category
placement here because the paper's evaluation criteria follow from
it: the deliverable is the validated map plus the measured
boundaries, and the appropriate comparison class is computational
studies of stress physiology, not wet-lab strain-engineering
programs.

Unusually for a modeling paper, this work carries an external

\subsection{On pre-registration and the economics of negative results}

We close the interpretive part of the paper with the methodological
claim we feel strongest, because this study is also an existence
proof for it. Computational modeling has an asymmetry that
experimental biology does not: the marginal cost of another run is
nearly zero, so the temptation to adjust after outcomes - retune a
parameter, relax a threshold, redefine a hit - is constant, invisible,
and undetectable in the published record. The defenses are
organizational, not mathematical: lock the design before outcomes,
hash the lock, amend only forward, and score the gates as they fall.
This program did all four, and the consequence is on the front page:
three of four gates did not pass, the validation is a null, and the
paper is better for it, because the surviving positive (the
co-limitation mechanism) is now the output of a process that was
demonstrably willing to print its own failures. A positive result
from a process that never shows a negative carries no information
about its own reliability; a positive result from this one carries
the ledger with it.

The negative results themselves have an economics worth stating
plainly. The strain-design null (G4) is a map of where not to spend:
every knockout and scaling the campaign evaluated is one an
experimental program no longer needs to build. The interaction
fragility (G2) is a warning about a statistic: Bliss-style
non-additivity fractions in this model class are parameter-shaped,
and any future paper quoting one without a parameter sweep should be
asked for the sweep. The validation null (A6) is a boundary on a
method: flux balance with environment caps does not see the
gene-level stress biology that deletion screens measure, so its
predictions should be consumed at the process layer where this paper
validates it, and not at the genetic layer where it does not. None of
these required new experiments; all of them required the discipline
to run the losing comparisons and print them. We suggest that the
field's bottleneck in in silico strain design is no longer search
algorithms - it is the willingness to publish the ledger.

\subsection{Threats to validity, self-assessed}

Beyond the limitations the analysis produced
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion}), we record the threats we cannot
retire from inside the study. Construct validity: relative ethanol
yield is a flux ratio under a stated objective, and its mapping to
titer in an industrial batch is mediated by kinetics the model does
not carry; the A8 dynamic stage is the partial bridge, with its
confound declared. Internal validity: the collapse threshold (0.20)
and the sensitivity threshold (0.8) are locked but conventional, and
while gate decisions here are not threshold-fragile at the margins
that matter (the A5 null is total, the A6 null is degenerate), the
A2 collapse fractions move with the threshold by construction.
External validity: a single curated reconstruction of one species'
laboratory strain, however versioned, is not the diversity of
production strains; the qualitative mechanism (boundary co-limitation)
should transfer, the quantitative shadow prices should not be assumed
to. Conclusion validity: every inferential statement here rests on
locked statistics over finite samples (5{,}000 environments; 369
collapsed; 25 FVA-solved environments), and the FVA coverage gap is
the weakest of these. We regard the preregistration ledger as the
correct container for all four: the threats are named, quantified
where possible, and inseparable from the claims.

\subsection{Relation to empirical combined-stress studies}

The empirical literature on combined stress in production yeast, though
thinner than the single-stressor canon, frames the regime question this
paper answers in silico. Adaptive laboratory evolution under sequential
or paired stressors repeatedly recovers cross-protection - adaptation
to one stressor conferring tolerance to another - consistent with a
shared downstream bottleneck; our co-limitation result is the
constraint-level analog of that observation: two carbon gates whose
simultaneous closure looks, from the phenotype side, like one failure.
Industrial studies of heat waves during ethanol campaigns report
exactly the dose-accumulation phenomenology our A8 stage produces -
productivity losses that arrive before the temperature record crosses
any alarm threshold, and incomplete recovery after the wave passes -
which is why we describe the degree-hour formulation as operational
rather than academic. And the deletion-screen literature we validate
against (A6) is itself the empirical map of the layer our model
cannot see; reading our null and their hits together locates the
stress response's division of labor: carbon economics at the flux
layer, survival biology at the proteostasis layer, with the
transcriptional program (A7) as the cell's own bridge between them.

\subsection{What a fair critic should attack first}

In the spirit of the ledger, we name the weakest joints. A critic
should start with the environment mapping: the entire superstructure
rests on four declared functional forms, one of which (maintenance)
is unconstrained by direct measurement and one of which (the thermal
margin) demonstrably moves a gate. They should then attack the
objective choice - maximal ethanol exchange is industrial but not
unique, and a growth-coupled or ATP-yield objective would draw a
different, possibly more gene-visible, map. Third, the collapse
threshold and the 369-environment bottleneck subsample, where our own
FVA coverage (25 solved environments) is the thinnest evidence in the
paper. Fourth, the judge protocol: a single-question evaluation of a
long technical record, however archived, is a coarse instrument, and
we treat its verdict as one input among several, not a court. What we
ask the critic to grant is the ledger itself: given the locked
process, the failures are as much measurements as the successes, and
the whole of it - code, hashes, artifacts, verdicts - is available
for re-execution and disagreement.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We built a virtual \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} for climate-resilient
fermentation as a computational-biology study: a pinned consensus
genome-scale model, four declared stress layers, a locked 72{,}000-
environment grid, pre-registered analysis stages, binary gates, and
independent validation against accession-grade datasets. The program
did not find a climate-resilient strain, and it proved, exhaustively,
why it could not: in the combined-stress regime, collapse is co-limited
by an environment-imposed carbon supply cap and an obligatory
osmoregulatory carbon tax acting at the network boundary, where no
network-internal modification can reach. That mechanism - quantified,
re-verified, dual- and variability-supported, and concordant with the
cell's own transcriptional stress program - is the paper's positive
contribution. Around it stand three declared boundaries: interaction
quantification that is not parameter-robust, strain design that is
vacuous in this regime, and gene-level prediction that fails against
independent screens. We believe this is what a virtual cell model is
for: not to promise designs it cannot validate, but to map, honestly
and reproducibly, where the physics of the process lives. The map,
the boundaries, and the full audit trail are the deliverable.


\subsection{What we would tell a producer, a modeler, and a reviewer}

\emph{To the producer.} Your enemy in a heat wave is the integral,
not the peak. The model's most operational number is the one-hour gap
between dynamic collapse and the static boundary: a campaign run to
the edge of a static tolerance chart is, in model terms, already
failing before the chart says so. Cooling and feed decisions made
against accumulated thermal dose are the cheap win; strain
replacement is not the lever this analysis supports, and mash
osmolarity management is the underrated one, because the glycerol tax
binds in two of three solvable collapse scenarios.

\emph{To the modeler.} Boundary-imposed stress mappings are
self-certifying in a way network models are not: they produce clean
dose responses by construction, and every strain-design question
posed to them returns the boundary's answer. If your collapse
constraints are exchange bounds, your design stage is vacuous before
it runs - check where your constraints live before you budget the
search. And validate at the layer you claim: our process-level model
passes process-level checks and fails gene-level ones totally, and
both results were knowable only because both were run.

\emph{To the reviewer.} The three things most worth your skepticism
are the thermal margin (it moves a gate), the maintenance form (it is
assumed), and the FVA coverage (it is thin). Everything else in the
paper is either a locked definition, an artifact-backed number, or a
declared consequence of one of those three. The repository is
complete enough to attack all three directly, and the ledger format
means a successful attack improves the paper rather than
contradicting it.


\subsection{Program-level reflection}

This paper is one item's deliverable inside a larger program, and the
fit is worth stating because the program is the unit the discipline
serves. The item began, as all the program's items do, with an
ambitious owner-facing question - a climate-resilient fermentation
yeast - and a fixed contract: pre-register before compute, declare
negatives honestly, pad nothing, verify everything. What the contract
produced here is a case study in its own value. Without the locked
gates, the campaign would have presented the collapse map and the
mechanism and quietly dropped the design null and the validation
null - a weaker paper that looked stronger. With them, the item
closes with a complete account: what was asked, what was found, what
failed, what it means, and what comes next, every claim traceable to
an artifact and every artifact to a hash. The program-level lesson we
carry to the other items is that the redirect is not the failure
mode: the failure mode is the unreported redirect. An item that
changes direction under its own evidence and says so is the program
working as designed.

\subsection{Final summary}

A virtual \emph{S.\ cerevisiae}, built from a pinned public
reconstruction and four declared stress layers, was run through a
pre-registered eight-stage campaign over a 72{,}000-environment
design space and validated against accession-grade experimental
screens. It produced: a quantitative collapse map of the combined
heat--ethanol--osmotic regime; a validated co-limitation mechanism
(glucose-uptake capacity and the osmoregulatory glycerol tax, with
shadow prices 0.58 and 0.37) that names process-level intervention
targets; a parameter-fragility result that retires naive
stress-synergy statistics in this model class; an exhaustive,
baseline-certified null for network-internal strain design in this
regime; a degenerate, mechanistically explained null for gene-level
prediction against three independent deletion screens; directional
concordance with the cell's transcriptional stress program
(10/10 heat-shock markers, $p=9.8\times10^{-4}$); and a dynamic
degree-hour rule that moves collapse onset an hour ahead of static
expectation under heat waves. The gate ledger reads G1 partial, G2
failed, G3 met in substance, G4 failed. The program's judge archived
its verdict; the repository archives everything else. The organism
for climate-resilient fermentation was not found in this model; the
map of where to look, and of where not to bother, was.

\subsection{Closing statement}

The program this paper belongs to began by asking a virtual yeast to
design its own successor. It ends - for now - with something we judge
more valuable: a validated map of where fermentation productivity
lives and dies under the combined stresses of a warming production
environment, a quantified mechanism with named intervention targets,
an exhaustive demonstration of where the current method class cannot
go, and a public ledger in which every one of those claims carries
its evidence and its caveat on its face. The climate problem the
program started from has not gotten smaller. The tool this paper
delivers for it is not a strain; it is the knowledge of which levers
exist, which do not, and what it would take to build the ones that
should. That is the virtual yeast's first honest answer.
