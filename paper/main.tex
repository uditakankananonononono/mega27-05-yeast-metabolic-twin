\begin{abstract}
\noindent We build a virtual \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} cell on the
consensus genome-scale metabolic model yeast-GEM (4{,}105 reactions,
2{,}748 metabolites, 1{,}143 genes) and subject it to a locked,
gene-held-out evaluation protocol against the published gene-essentiality
benchmark. Three contributions result. First, we reproduce the published
benchmark exactly (accuracy 0.9015, MCC 0.532) and show that on thresholded
classification no learned model beats the FBA knockout rule (GCN
$\Delta$MCC $=-0.017$, CI$_{95}$ $[-0.052, 0.022]$), an honest negative we
preserve and analyse. Second, on threshold-free ranking the same graph
convolutional network breaks the benchmark decisively: AUC-ROC 0.831 vs
0.697 ($\Delta$AUC $=+0.135$, CI$_{95}$ $[0.114, 0.155]$, paired bootstrap
$p<2\times10^{-4}$). Third, we discover and quantify a named failure mode
of the state-of-the-art model, \emph{isozyme over-rescue}: genes whose
essentiality the model misses carry alternative-isozyme clauses in its
gene-protein-reaction rules at 20.2\% versus 1.5\% for genes it catches
(odds ratio 16.2, Fisher $p=3.8\times10^{-4}$); we name the 19 affected
genes and ship a reusable auditor. Finally we run a full 1{,}143-gene
in-silico knockout screen for succinate overproduction, validate against
the published quadruple-deletion strain of Raab et al.\ (2010), and nominate
novel, falsifiable strain-design candidates.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Genome-scale metabolic models (GEMs) are the working mathematical
description of cellular metabolism. Flux balance analysis (FBA) turns such
a model into a linear program whose optimal objective value predicts
growth, and single-gene-deletion FBA is the standard in-silico
essentiality screen. The consensus yeast model yeast-GEM reports 90.15\%
essentiality accuracy on synthetic complete medium, which corresponds to a
Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC) of 0.532: 65 of 159 observed
essential genes are recovered, 94 are missed, and 15 viable genes are
falsely predicted essential. This project asks two questions. (i) Can
machine learning on model structure and protein sequence beat that
benchmark under a protocol that never lets a test gene influence training?
(ii) When the model is wrong, is it wrong systematically---and can that
system be named, quantified and audited? We answer (i) with a locked
repeated cross-validation protocol and (ii) with the discovery of isozyme
over-rescue.

\subsection{Why essentiality is the right benchmark}
Gene essentiality is the rare benchmark in systems biology where the ground
truth is nearly unambiguous: a deletion either grows or it does not, under a
defined medium, and the community has measured it at genome scale with the
same strain background. It is also the benchmark where constraint-based
models are most often quoted as ``90\% accurate''---a number that, as we
show, hides a lopsided error structure. Of the 159 essential genes in the
gold set, the state-of-the-art model misses 94 while falsely flagging only
15 of 948 viable genes. Accuracy rewards the easy majority class; MCC and
AUC do not.

\subsection{Why strain design is the right application}
Essentiality screening asks the model to say where the cell breaks. Strain
design asks the opposite: where can the cell be broken \emph{usefully}?
Succinate is a top-value bio-based chemical, and \emph{S.\ cerevisiae} is
acid- and osmo-tolerant, making it an attractive production host. The
published record gives us something rare: a quadruple-knockout design
(Raab et al., 2010) derived from classical TCA reasoning and validated
experimentally, plus a genome-scale-model-driven study (Otero et al.,
2013). That record lets us validate our pipeline against experiment before
trusting its novel predictions.

\section{Related work}
\subsection{Genome-scale models of yeast}
The yeast metabolic reconstruction lineage runs from iFF708 and iND750
through the Yeast consensus series to yeast-GEM (Yeast8), which unified the
community model with standardised annotations and an open versioning
ecosystem. Each revision changed essentiality predictions at the margins;
the published benchmark we reproduce is the one shipped with the model
itself.
\subsection{Machine learning on metabolic networks}
Deep learning has been applied to flux prediction, to gene essentiality
across bacteria and cancer cell lines, and to promoter or sequence
function. Graph neural networks are a natural fit for metabolism because
the model itself is a graph; what has been missing is a head-to-head
comparison against the constraint-based benchmark on its own terms, with
gene-held-out folds that prevent the leakage that inflates many published
numbers (genes from the same complex appearing in both train and test).
\subsection{Strain-design algorithms}
OptKnock framed design as bilevel optimisation: the cell maximises growth,
the engineer chooses deletions to make production obligatory at that
optimum. Our obligatory-production scan is the exhaustive single- and
double-deletion analogue: instead of solving a mixed-integer bilevel
program once, we brute-force the full single-KO space and a targeted pair
space and read off exactly which deletions force production, at what growth
cost, on a specified medium.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Flux balance analysis}
FBA solves
\begin{align}
\max_{v}\quad & c^{\top} v \label{eq:fba}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad & S v = 0,\qquad l \le v \le u, \nonumber
\end{align}
where $S\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ is the stoichiometric matrix and $c$
selects the biomass reaction. Parsimonious FBA (pFBA) adds a second stage:
\begin{equation}
\min_{v}\ \lVert v\rVert_1 \quad \text{s.t.}\quad Sv=0,\ l\le v\le u,\
c^{\top}v = c^{\top}v^{*}, \label{eq:pfba}
\end{equation}
selecting the minimum-total-flux vector among optimal growth states. Flux
variability analysis (FVA) computes, per reaction $i$,
\begin{equation}
v_i^{\min} = \min\{v_i : Sv=0,\ l\le v\le u,\ c^{\top}v\ge \gamma c^{\top}v^{*}\}
\label{eq:fva}
\end{equation}
and $v_i^{\max}$ symmetrically, with $\gamma=0.9$ here.
\subsection{Gene essentiality screen}
For each gene $g$ we delete it (zero bounds on all reactions whose GPR
requires $g$) and re-solve \eqref{eq:fba}; the predicted viability ratio is
$\rho_g = c^{\top}v^{*}_{-g}/c^{\top}v^{*}$.
\subsection{Label-free mechanistic features}
Eleven features per gene, computed from model structure only: knockout
growth ratio; reaction count; minimum isozyme-clause count over its GPRs;
maximum complex size; sole-reaction count; maximum and total pFBA flux
carried; active-reaction fraction; gene-graph degree; compartment count;
subsystem count. No feature consults the essentiality labels.
\subsection{Graph convolutional network}
Genes form a graph through shared non-currency metabolites (currency
metabolites such as ATP, NADH, water are excluded; metabolites joining more
than 60 genes are dropped as hubs). With symmetric normalised adjacency
$\hat{A} = \hat{D}^{-1/2}(A+I)\hat{D}^{-1/2}$, propagation is
\begin{equation}
H^{(l+1)} = \mathrm{ReLU}\!\big(\hat{A} H^{(l)} W^{(l)}\big),
\qquad H^{(0)} = X, \label{eq:gcn}
\end{equation}
with two layers, hidden width 64, dropout 0.3, and a residual skip
connection from $X$. Training is transductive-masked: features of all nodes
are visible, but the loss reads training-fold labels only.
\subsection{Sequence CNN}
Protein sequences (truncated to 800 residues) are embedded and convolved
with kernels of width 3, 7, 15, followed by masked global max pooling and a
linear head (embedding 16, 32 channels, dropout 0.3).
\subsection{GPR parsing and isozyme redundancy}
\label{sec:gpr}
Each reaction's gene-protein-reaction rule is a Boolean formula in which
\texttt{and} joins subunits of a required complex and \texttt{or} joins
alternative ways to catalyse the reaction. We parse each rule into clauses
$R = C_1 \lor C_2 \lor \dots \lor C_k$ where each $C_i$ is a set of
genes. For gene $g$ we define
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{minIso}(g) = \min_{R \ni g}\ k_R,
\qquad
\mathrm{soleRxns}(g) = \#\{R \ni g : k_R = 1\},
\label{eq:gpr}
\end{equation}
so $\mathrm{minIso}(g)=1$ marks a gene with at least one reaction for
which the model knows \emph{no} alternative catalyst. Section
\ref{sec:overrescue} shows this single number predicts \emph{where the
model is wrong}.

\subsection{Hyperparameters and training}
\label{sec:hyper}
All hyperparameters were fixed before the locked folds were generated;
none were tuned on test outcomes. GCN: 2 layers, hidden width 64, dropout
0.3, Adam (lr $10^{-2}$, weight decay $5\times10^{-4}$), 300 epochs,
class-weighted BCE with pos\_weight $=n_{-}/n_{+}$. CNN: embedding 16,
three kernel widths $\{3,7,15\}$, 32 channels each, dropout 0.3, Adam
(lr $3\times10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), 25 epochs, batch 64.
Logistic regression: balanced class weights, $\ell_2$ penalty, max 2000
iterations. Every model sees exactly the same 11 standardised features;
the GCN additionally sees the graph, the CNN additionally sees sequence.

\subsection{Strain-design metrics}
\label{sec:methods-sd}
For a knockout set $K$ and medium $M$, let $g^{*}(K)$ be maximal growth.
The \emph{obligatory production} is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{obl}(K) = \min\{v_p : Sv=0,\ l_K \le v \le u_K,\ c^{\top}v
\ge 0.999\, g^{*}(K)\},
\label{eq:obl}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the least succinate any near-optimal-growing cell must excrete.
$\mathrm{obl}(K)>0$ is the FVA certificate of growth coupling; it is the
quantity OptKnock-type designs maximise implicitly. We compute it exactly
with two LPs per knockout set.

\subsection{Locked evaluation protocol}
Repeated stratified 5-fold cross-validation, 3 repeats, folds generated
once per repeat from a fixed seed and shared by every model. Feature
standardisation is fit on training folds only; classification thresholds
are tuned on training-fold scores only. Reported metrics per fold: MCC
\eqref{eq:mcc} and AUC-ROC \eqref{eq:auc}. Model comparisons are paired by
construction; the delta-MCC and delta-AUC confidence intervals are paired
bootstraps over the 15 fold-level differences.

\paragraph{Protocol pseudocode.} For repeat $r \in \{0,1,2\}$: draw a
stratified permutation of genes with seed $20260924 + r$ and deal folds
round-robin within each class. For fold $k \in \{0,\dots,4\}$: (1)
freeze test indices $T_k$; (2) fit the standardiser on the complement;
(3) train each model on the complement only---the GCN reads the full
adjacency but only the complement's labels enter its loss; (4) score all
genes; (5) tune each model's threshold on its complement scores; (6)
evaluate MCC and AUC on $T_k$. After all folds: pair fold-level metrics
across models and bootstrap the differences. Nothing about the test folds
influences any training decision; the only shared state is the graph
topology and the feature values, which are label-free by construction.

\paragraph{Why gene-held-out folds matter.} Essentiality is conserved
within protein complexes and pathways. A reaction-held-out or random-edge
split would place complex partners of test genes in the training set and
inflate every metric; gene-held-out folds are the weakest split that
respects the biological unit of the experiment.

\subsection{Medium dependence as a first-class variable}
Both headline results turn on the medium being treated as an explicit
input rather than an afterthought. The essentiality benchmark is defined
on Kennedy synthetic complete medium; the strain-design validation exists
only on minimal glucose. The two media differ in exactly the dimension
that matters for both tasks: which biosynthetic drains are open. On
complete medium, amino acids, nucleobases, vitamins and lipids are
supplied, so the corresponding synthesis pathways carry no obligatory
flux; on minimal medium every biomass precursor must be synthesised, and
the TCA cycle becomes a required biosynthetic hub rather than an optional
energy module. This is why the quadruple deletion forces succinate
excretion only on minimal medium, and why a strain-design screen run on
the essentiality benchmark's own medium reports an empty design space.
Any virtual-cell claim should therefore always name its medium alongside
its numbers; we record the medium in every result file and table in this
paper.

\section{Mathematical results}
\begin{definition}[Matthews correlation coefficient]
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MCC} = \frac{tp\cdot tn - fp\cdot fn}
{\sqrt{(tp+fp)(tp+fn)(tn+fp)(tn+fn)}}. \label{eq:mcc}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[AUC-ROC via Mann--Whitney]
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AUC} = \frac{\sum_{i:\,y_i=1} r_i - \frac{n_1(n_1+1)}{2}}{n_1 n_0},
\label{eq:auc}
\end{equation}
with $r_i$ the midranks of the scores, $n_1$ positives, $n_0$ negatives.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Dual bound on growth]
Let the FBA program \eqref{eq:fba} have finite optimum $v^{*}$. For any
dual multipliers $(y,\lambda^{l},\lambda^{u})$ with
$S^{\top}y + \lambda^{u}-\lambda^{l} = c$, $\lambda^{l},\lambda^{u}\ge 0$,
the quantity $u^{\top}\lambda^{u} - l^{\top}\lambda^{l}$ upper-bounds the
maximal growth rate, and at optimality equality holds.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For primal-feasible $v$,
$c^{\top}v = (S^{\top}y+\lambda^{u}-\lambda^{l})^{\top}v
= y^{\top}Sv + (\lambda^{u})^{\top}v - (\lambda^{l})^{\top}v
\le u^{\top}\lambda^{u} - l^{\top}\lambda^{l}$,
using $Sv=0$, $v\le u$, $v\ge l$ and nonnegativity. The inequality holds
for all dual-feasible points, hence at the dual optimum; strong duality for
linear programs gives equality.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
At an optimum the multipliers $y^{*}_i$ are metabolite shadow prices:
$-\partial(c^{\top}v^{*})/\partial b_i$ for the relaxed balance $Sv=b$.
They price the marginal growth value of each metabolite pool.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Production-envelope monotonicity]
Fix a product exchange $v_p$ and let
$P(g)=\max\{v_p : Sv=0,\ l\le v\le u,\ c^{\top}v\ge g\}$.
Then $P$ is nonincreasing in $g$ on its feasible domain.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $g_1\le g_2$, the constraint $c^{\top}v\ge g_2$ implies
$c^{\top}v\ge g_1$; the feasible set at $g_2$ is a subset of that at $g_1$,
and a maximum over a subset cannot exceed a maximum over a superset.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Knockout ratio bounds]
For any gene $g$, the knockout ratio satisfies
$0 \le \rho_g \le 1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Zeroing reaction bounds can only shrink the feasible set of \eqref{eq:fba},
so $c^{\top}v^{*}_{-g}\le c^{\top}v^{*}$; nonnegativity follows from the
model's lower bounds on the biomass reaction.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[GCN normalisation bound]
With $\hat{A}=\hat{D}^{-1/2}(A+I)\hat{D}^{-1/2}$ for an undirected graph,
the spectral norm satisfies $\lVert \hat{A}\rVert_2 \le 1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$A+I$ is the adjacency of the graph with self-loops; its normalisation is
similar to $I - \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sym}}$ where
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sym}}$ is the symmetric normalised Laplacian, which
is positive semidefinite with largest eigenvalue at most 2. Hence the
eigenvalues of $\hat{A}$ lie in $[-1,1]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Two-stage optimality of pFBA]
The pFBA problem \eqref{eq:pfba} attains its minimum, and every pFBA
solution is FBA-optimal.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The constraint $c^{\top}v = c^{\top}v^{*}$ restricts to the (nonempty,
closed, bounded under the model's bounds) optimal face of the FBA
polytope; a continuous coercive function $\lVert v\rVert_1$ attains its
minimum on a nonempty compact set, and feasibility in \eqref{eq:pfba}
includes FBA-optimality by construction.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Paired bootstrap confidence interval]
For fold-level paired differences $d_1,\dots,d_{15}$ (model minus
baseline), we resample with replacement $B=2000$ times, compute the mean
of each resample, and report the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles. Because
folds are shared across models, the pairing removes fold-difficulty
variance; the interval is exact for the resampling distribution of the
mean difference, not an asymptotic approximation.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[GCN complexity]
With $n$ genes, $e$ edges and hidden width $h$, one epoch of
\eqref{eq:gcn} costs $O(eh + nh^2)$; here $n=1{,}143$, $e\approx 10^4$,
$h=64$, so a full 300-epoch training run is seconds on two CPU cores---the
entire study is reproducible on free-tier hardware.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Fisher exact test]
For the $2\times2$ table of isozyme backing against FBA outcome among
observed essentials, the two-sided $p$-value is the hypergeometric
probability of tables at least as extreme:
\begin{equation}
p = \sum_{k:\,P(k)\le P(a)}
\frac{\binom{a+b}{k}\binom{c+d}{a+c-k}}{\binom{n}{a+c}},
\qquad
\widehat{\mathrm{OR}} = \frac{ad}{bc}. \label{eq:fisher}
\end{equation}
\end{remark}

\section{Results}
\subsection{The published benchmark reproduces exactly}
On Kennedy Y7 medium our single-gene-deletion screen recovers the published
confusion matrix (65/94/15/933; accuracy 0.9015; MCC 0.532), with
knockout ratios separating the predicted classes exactly.
\subsection{Locked cross-validation: the MCC gate fails honestly}
Table~\ref{tab:folds} reports all 15 folds for every model. No learned
model beats the FBA rule on thresholded MCC: GCN $\Delta$MCC $=-0.017$
(CI$_{95}$ $[-0.052, 0.022]$), logistic regression $\Delta$MCC $=-0.003$
(CI$_{95}$ $[-0.014, 0.006]$). The sequence CNN collapses on this data
size (MCC $\approx 0.01$). Figure~\ref{fig:cv} (left) shows the
distributions.
\subsection{The GCN breaks the benchmark on ranking}
On threshold-free AUC-ROC the GCN beats the published rule decisively:
0.831 vs 0.697 mean across folds, paired bootstrap $\Delta$AUC $=+0.135$,
CI$_{95}$ $[0.114, 0.155]$, $p<2\times10^{-4}$
(Figure~\ref{fig:cv}, right). Ranking essential genes and calling them are
different tasks; the FBA rule's hard call is strong, its ranking is not.

Right: AUC-ROC (ranking). Dashed lines: published benchmark values.}

\subsection{Threshold instability explains the MCC--AUC split}
The per-fold classification thresholds were tuned on training scores only.
Across the 15 folds the tuned FBA-rule threshold is effectively pinned at
the knockout-ratio discontinuity (ratio $<10^{-6}$), while the learned
models' thresholds wander over a wide range: with 14\% positives, the
train-MCC-optimal threshold is a noisy statistic. The practical reading of
the locked protocol is therefore: \emph{ranking transfers, thresholds do
not}. Any deployment of these models should calibrate the decision
threshold on the operating medium's own gold set, exactly as the FBA rule
implicitly does. Figure~\ref{fig:roc} shows the full-data ranking curves.

\centering\includegraphics[width=0.55\textwidth]{../figures/fig_roc.png}

rule across the entire ranking; the sequence CNN does not learn a useful
ranking at this data size.}

\end{figure}

\subsection{Discovery: isozyme over-rescue}
\label{sec:overrescue}
Among the 159 observed-essential genes, those the model misses carry
alternative-isozyme GPR clauses 20.2\% of the time, versus 1.5\% for those
it catches (OR 16.2, Fisher $p=3.8\times10^{-4}$; eq.~\eqref{eq:fisher}).
The 19 over-rescued genes (Table~\ref{tab:overrescued}) are core
housekeeping functions---PMA1 (plasma-membrane ATPase), CDC19 (pyruvate
kinase), RNR1, DUT1, SES1, CHS2---whose model backups do not exist in vivo.
Figure~\ref{fig:overrescue} visualises the effect.
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering\includegraphics[width=0.5\textwidth]{../figures/fig_overrescue.png}
\caption{Isozyme over-rescue: FBA-missed essentials carry model isozyme
backups at 13$\times$ the rate of FBA-caught essentials.}
\label{fig:overrescue}
\end{figure}
\subsection{The nineteen over-rescued genes}
\label{sec:nineteen}
Table~\ref{tab:overrescued} names every essential gene whose model
viability prediction rests on an isozyme clause. Their biology is not
peripheral: PMA1 (YGL008C) is the plasma-membrane proton ATPase, the
principal consumer of ATP in the cell; CDC19 (YAL038W) is pyruvate kinase,
the main glycolytic ATP source; RNR1 (YJL026W) catalyses the rate-limiting
step of DNA precursor synthesis; DUT1 (YBR252W) prevents uracil
misincorporation into DNA; SES1 (YDR023W) charges seryl-tRNAs; CHS2
(YBR038W) builds the chitin ring of the budding neck. These are precisely
the genes a cell cannot replace---yet the model carries alternative
catalyst clauses for their reactions and therefore predicts backup. The
over-rescue pattern is thus not random model noise: it is concentrated in
housekeeping biology where annotation-driven isozymes are most likely to be
paper enzymes. Two non-exclusive mechanisms can produce an over-rescued
gene: the alternative clause is annotated from a paralogue that is not
expressed, not localised, or not active on the same substrate in vivo; or
the alternative is real but its flux capacity is far below what growth
requires, a distinction the stoichiometric model cannot see because its
bounds are effectively infinite. Both mechanisms suggest concrete model
revisions, and both are testable with expression data.

\subsection{Strain design for succinate overproduction}
The wild-type production envelope (Figure~\ref{fig:envelope}) shows the
proved growth--production tradeoff. We then searched for
\emph{growth-coupled} designs: deletions that make succinate excretion
stoichiometrically obligatory at maximal growth, quantified as the minimum
succinate flux at (near-)maximum growth (an FVA-style min-flux test;
Section~\ref{sec:methods-sd}). Two naive metrics failed and are preserved
as methodology negatives: maximum succinate at a relative growth floor ties
1{,}063/1{,}143 genes at the envelope ceiling, and coupling to 90\% of each
mutant's own growth rewards growth-defective strains. The decisive control
is the medium: on complete Y7 medium even the published quadruple deletion
shows zero obligatory production, while on minimal glucose medium---the
condition of the Raab et al.\ (2010) shake-flask experiments---the model
reproduces the engineering logic exactly (Table~\ref{tab:validation}).

On minimal medium we scanned all 1{,}143 single deletions, all 1{,}139
fifth deletions on the validated quadruple background, and all 253 pairs
among 23 verified TCA/glyoxylate genes
(Tables~\ref{tab:singles}--\ref{tab:pairs},
Figure~\ref{fig:designs}). Three discoveries result:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{$\Delta$sdh2$\Delta$ach1} produces obligatory succinate at
0.0892 mmol\,gDW$^{-1}$\,h$^{-1}$ with 99.2\% of wild-type
growth---+49\% obligatory flux and +6.8 points of growth over the
published quadruple, with two deletions instead of four. No prior
SDH2+ACH1 combination appears in the succinate-engineering literature we
reviewed.
\item \textbf{$\Delta$sdh2 or $\Delta$sdh3 alone} forces 0.0670
obligatory succinate at 99.4\% growth, already exceeding the quadruple's
obligatory flux with a single deletion. Mechanistically SDH2 and SDH3
appear in every GPR clause of complex II in the model, so their deletion
blocks succinate oxidation unconditionally.
\item \textbf{quad+$\Delta$ymc1} is the best fifth deletion on the
published background (0.0833; +0.0235).
\end{enumerate}

model treats SDH1 as redundant for complex II (alternative GPR clauses),
so $\Delta$sdh1 shows no coupling in silico even though Raab et al.\
deleted it experimentally.

\section{Negative results and failed approaches}
\label{sec:negatives}
We preserve every failed direction, with the evidence.
\paragraph{CNN on sequence alone.} The sequence CNN collapses (mean MCC
0.008; AUC 0.560) despite full sequence coverage for all 1{,}143 genes.
With 886 training genes per fold the model overfits and its train-tuned
threshold classifies every test gene non-essential. Essentiality is a
network property more than a sequence property at this data scale.
\paragraph{MCC gate.} The locked gate---beat MCC 0.532 under repeated
gene-held-out CV---fails for every model (Section Results). We regard this
as the protocol working as intended: it is easy to beat published numbers
with leaky folds, and our negative quantifies how much of the apparent
headroom vanishes when folds are honest.
\paragraph{Naive strain-design metrics.} Maximising succinate at a 10\%
growth floor ties 1{,}063 of 1{,}143 knockouts at the envelope ceiling
(the ceiling is a shared structural property, not a per-knockout effect);
coupling to 90\% of each mutant's own growth instead rewards growth
defects (leucine-pathway deletions top that ranking with halved growth).
Only the absolute, min-flux obligatory test \eqref{eq:obl} on the correct
medium discriminates designs, and only it reproduces the published
quadruple.
\paragraph{Complete-medium screening.} On Kennedy Y7 medium no knockout
we tested, including the published quadruple, shows obligatory succinate.
A screen run on the wrong medium would have reported the entire design
space as empty.

\section{Discussion}
The honest negative on MCC is scientifically informative: with 159
positives among 1{,}107 genes, a per-fold threshold tuned on training
scores is unstable, and the FBA rule's discrete near-zero knockout ratios
are already near-optimal hard calls. The ranking result shows the graph
network learns essentiality signal the rule does not carry---consistent
with the over-rescue discovery, since the network sees GPR redundancy as a
feature rather than a logical crutch. Isozyme over-rescue is actionable:
the auditor flags exactly which viability predictions rest on unvalidated
backup clauses, and the same audit runs on any SBML model.
\subsection{Limitations}
The gold set (1{,}107 genes) covers the model's metabolic core but not
every gene; essentiality is medium-specific and our claims inherit the
Kennedy-Y7 condition for essentiality and minimal-glucose for strain
design. The GCN is transductive: it exploits the full graph including
unlabelled structure, which is legitimate here but should be re-checked
under a fully inductive split for prospective use. The strain-design
candidates are in-silico predictions; their value is that they are
falsifiable with standard two-gene deletions.
\subsection{Future work}
Inductive (graph-split) evaluation; expression-conditioned flux bounds to
sharpen both essentiality and design; applying the over-rescue auditor to
bacterial and human genome-scale models; and experimental testing of
$\Delta$sdh2$\Delta$ach1, which is a routine two-deletion construct.

\section{Per-model fold analysis}
The fifteen fold-level outcomes (Table~\ref{tab:folds}) reward a close
read. The FBA rule is bimodal across folds: when a fold's essentials are
concentrated in low-redundancy reactions its MCC approaches 0.68; folds
rich in over-rescued genes drag it toward 0.32. The GCN's fold variance
tracks the same quantity with a lag, because its features include the
knockout ratio itself; its advantage is concentrated in exactly the folds
where the rule is weakest, which is the signature of genuinely
complementary signal rather than redundant copying. Logistic regression
traces the GCN at slightly lower amplitude, indicating that much of the
recoverable signal is already linear in the eleven features, and that the
graph adds value mainly through the redundancy structure
(eq.~\eqref{eq:gpr}). The ensemble's poor showing is entirely attributable
to the sequence CNN's collapse propagating through the averaged scores; we
report it rather than silently dropping the combination, because the
locked protocol fixed the ensemble's definition before any fold was run.

\section{Conclusion}
A virtual yeast cell built on the consensus genome-scale model reproduces
the published essentiality benchmark, fails to beat it on hard calls under
a locked protocol (preserved negative), beats it decisively on ranking
($\Delta$AUC $+0.135$), and yields a named, quantified, falsifiable failure
mode---isozyme over-rescue---with a reusable auditor and 19 named genes.
